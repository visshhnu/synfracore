# CA Articleship

## What Articleship Is

Articleship is a mandatory **3-year practical training period** under a practicing Chartered Accountant, required by ICAI between (or overlapping) the Intermediate and Final levels. It's what makes the CA qualification practice-ready rather than purely theoretical — you can't become a CA without completing it.

## Registering for Articleship

1. **Eligibility**: Clear at least one group of CA Intermediate (both groups not required to start).
2. **ICITSS**: Complete the Integrated Course on Information Technology and Soft Skills (a short mandatory course) before registering.
3. **Find a principal**: A practicing CA or CA firm willing to take you on — through campus placement, direct application, or referrals.
4. **Register with ICAI**: Submit Form 102/103 within 30 days of starting articleship, along with the deed of articles signed by you and your principal.

## What the 3 Years Actually Look Like

| Year | Typical exposure |
|---|---|
| Year 1 | Audit assistance, vouching, basic compliance filings, learning firm workflows |
| Year 2 | More independent audit work, direct client interaction, tax return preparation |
| Year 3 | Leading small audit engagements, GST/income tax assessments, exposure to advisory work |

## AICITSS

Before appearing for CA Final, you must also complete **AICITSS** (Advanced ICITSS) — Advanced IT and Management Communication Skills — typically during the last year of articleship.

## Common Practical Questions

- **Can I change my principal mid-articleship?** Yes, under specific ICAI-permitted conditions (transfer provisions), but it resets some continuity requirements — check current ICAI rules before switching.
- **Can I study for Intermediate/Final while doing articleship?** Yes — this is the norm, not the exception. Most successful candidates study evenings/weekends throughout articleship rather than treating it as a separate phase.
- **Stipend**: ICAI mandates a minimum stipend that increases by city classification and year of articleship — it is modest, and most articled clerks treat it as training compensation, not a living wage.

:::tip
Firms that give you real audit and tax exposure (not just data entry) are worth more to your long-term CA career than firms offering a slightly higher stipend with narrow work — choose your principal accordingly.
:::
