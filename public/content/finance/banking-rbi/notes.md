# Career Track — Analyst vs Relationship Manager

## Two Different Jobs, Same Starting Point

Banking and finance graduates commonly land in one of two front-line roles after the foundational RBI/banking-framework knowledge covered elsewhere on this page — and they're genuinely different day-to-day jobs, not just different titles for the same work.

## Credit / Equity Analyst

- **What the work actually is**: research-facing. Building financial models, reading balance sheets and cash flow statements, assessing a borrower's or company's creditworthiness, writing credit notes or equity research reports.
- **Day-to-day**: heavy on Excel/financial modelling, industry research, ratio analysis (current ratio, debt-to-equity, interest coverage), and comparing a borrower/company against sector peers.
- **Who they report to / work with**: credit committees, risk teams, portfolio managers — output is a written recommendation, not a client conversation.
- **Career ladder**: Analyst → Senior Analyst → Associate Vice President → Vice President (Credit/Risk).

## Relationship Manager (RM)

- **What the work actually is**: client-facing. Acquiring and retaining banking relationships (retail, SME, or corporate depending on the desk), cross-selling products (loans, deposits, insurance, investment products), and being the client's single point of contact.
- **Day-to-day**: client meetings and calls, sales targets (AUM growth, new accounts, product penetration), coordinating internally with credit/ops teams to get a client's request processed.
- **Who they report to / work with**: branch/zonal sales heads — output is measured in relationship value (AUM, revenue) and client satisfaction, not written analysis.
- **Career ladder**: RM → Senior RM → Branch Manager / Zonal Manager (sales & people-management track).

## Which One Fits You

- Prefer research, modelling, and writing over sales conversations → Analyst track.
- Prefer client relationships, targets, and business development over deep-dive modelling → RM track.
- Both tracks start from the same foundational knowledge (banking structure, RBI regulation, credit basics) — the fork happens at your first posting, not before, so the framework knowledge on this page is common ground for either path.

## Compensation Reality Check

RM roles typically carry a variable/incentive component tied to targets on top of base pay; Analyst roles are usually fixed-pay-heavy with bonus tied to review cycles rather than monthly targets — a meaningful difference to factor in when choosing between the two.
