# CA Final — Previous Year Question Patterns

Representative question patterns reflecting CA Final's application-based, often cross-subject question style — illustrating the applied reasoning covered across this section, not a substitute for solving official ICAI question papers directly.

**Q1 (Financial Reporting pattern — multi-standard integration).** A parent company acquires 80% of a subsidiary, with the purchase consideration including a deferred payment contingent on the subsidiary achieving specific future profit targets. Identify the multiple accounting standards this single scenario likely engages, and why.
This scenario likely engages: the business combination standard (Ind AS 103, for the acquisition itself and determining goodwill), a standard governing financial instruments/contingent consideration measurement (for the deferred, contingent payment's fair value at acquisition and subsequent remeasurement), consolidation standards (for preparing consolidated financial statements), and potentially deferred tax standards (Ind AS 12, if the acquisition creates temporary differences between accounting and tax bases). Correctly identifying that all these standards interact within one scenario — not just applying the business combination standard in isolation — is the genuine CA Final-level skill being tested.

**Q2 (AFM pattern — combined technique application).** A company is evaluating an overseas project financed with a mix of debt and equity, where the debt is denominated in the foreign currency and a real option exists to abandon the project after year 1 if initial results are unfavorable. What distinct AFM techniques does this problem require combining?
This requires combining: WACC calculation reflecting the specific debt-equity financing mix (with appropriate cost of debt/equity), foreign currency considerations (translating foreign cash flows and/or the foreign-currency-denominated debt appropriately, potentially considering hedging), and real options valuation for the embedded abandonment option (which has genuine value distinct from a simple NPV calculation that ignores the flexibility to abandon). A candidate who applies only standard NPV/WACC without recognizing and separately valuing the abandonment option's real optionality would understate the project's true value.

**Q3 (Advanced Auditing pattern — complex entity application).** An auditor is engaged to audit a group with several subsidiaries, some audited by different component auditors, and the group includes an entity holding significant financial assets measured at fair value with limited observable market data. Identify the relevant Standards on Auditing this scenario invokes.
This scenario invokes SA 600 (Using the Work of Another Auditor, for the group audit involving component auditors — addressing coordination, reliance, and responsibility considerations) and SA 540 (Auditing Accounting Estimates, given the fair value measurement with limited observable market data, which involves significant management judgment and estimation uncertainty requiring specific auditor scrutiny beyond routine verification). Recognizing that a single audit engagement can require applying multiple SAs together, each addressing a different specific complexity in the same engagement, is the tested application skill.

**Q4 (Cross-subject pattern — Financial Reporting + Indirect Tax).** A company enters a sale-and-leaseback arrangement for a piece of equipment. Discuss both (a) the correct Ind AS treatment of this arrangement, and (b) the GST implications of the sale and subsequent lease payments.
This question deliberately spans two subject areas within one scenario: part (a) requires correctly applying the leases standard (assessing whether the arrangement qualifies as a genuine sale under the relevant criteria, and the resulting accounting treatment for both the seller-lessee and buyer-lessor), while part (b) requires separately analyzing the GST treatment of the sale transaction itself and the ongoing lease payments as a distinct supply. A strong answer addresses both parts with genuinely correct, subject-specific analysis rather than conflating the accounting and tax treatments or assuming one automatically determines the other.

**Q5 (Paper 6 pattern — integrated case).** A mid-sized manufacturing company is considering acquiring a smaller competitor, financed through a mix of retained earnings and new debt, with the target company operating in a different state with different applicable indirect tax registration requirements. Outline the distinct analytical threads this case requires addressing together.
A strong Paper 6-style answer addresses: strategic rationale (does the acquisition make sense given competitive positioning and market conditions — not assumed, but analyzed from the case facts), valuation and financing analysis (appropriate valuation method for the target, and the financial statement/leverage implications of the specific financing mix chosen), tax structuring considerations (the most tax-efficient way to structure the acquisition, and cross-state indirect tax registration/compliance implications given the target's different state), and legal/regulatory considerations (company law compliance for the transaction structure chosen). Critically, these threads inform each other — the financing mix chosen affects both the financial risk profile AND the tax treatment, and recognizing these interdependencies rather than addressing each thread in complete isolation is the genuine integration skill Paper 6 specifically tests.
