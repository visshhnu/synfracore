# CA/CS/CMA Foundation — Intermediate

## Business law — contract law fundamentals

The **Indian Contract Act, 1872** forms the foundational legal framework tested across all three institutes' Foundation business-law papers: a valid contract requires **offer and acceptance** (one party proposes terms, the other accepts them), **consideration** (something of value exchanged by each party — a contract generally isn't enforceable without it), **capacity to contract** (parties must be legally competent — of sound mind, of legal age, not disqualified by law), and **free consent** (agreement not obtained through coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, or mistake). Foundation-level questions frequently test whether a described scenario satisfies all these elements — a contract missing any one element (like consideration, or genuine free consent) may be void or voidable, and correctly identifying which specific element is missing (not just recognizing "something is wrong") is the actual tested skill.

## Void vs. voidable contracts

A frequently tested distinction: a **void** contract is one with no legal effect from the outset (or that becomes unenforceable due to a fundamental defect, like an agreement with a minor, who lacks legal capacity to contract) — it simply cannot be enforced by either party. A **voidable** contract is initially valid but can be canceled ("avoided") at the option of the party whose consent wasn't genuinely free (say, a contract entered under undue influence) — the aggrieved party has a choice to enforce or void it, unlike a void contract, which offers no such enforcement option to either party. This distinction matters because Foundation exam scenarios frequently test whether a student correctly identifies a contract as void or merely voidable, since the practical legal consequences differ meaningfully between the two.

## Business law — company law basics

Foundation-level company law introduces foundational concepts: a **company** is a distinct legal entity separate from its shareholders (the principle of **separate legal personality**, meaning the company can own property, enter contracts, and be sued in its own name, distinct from its owners), and shareholders generally have **limited liability** (their financial exposure is limited to their investment in the company, not extending to their personal assets for the company's debts) — this separate-personality and limited-liability combination is precisely what makes the company structure attractive for raising capital from investors who wouldn't otherwise be willing to accept unlimited personal liability for a business's obligations.

## Applying contract law concepts to business scenarios

Connecting the contract-law elements (above) to realistic Foundation exam scenarios: a scenario describing a business agreement where one party was a minor tests capacity to contract (the agreement is void, since minors lack contractual capacity under the Indian Contract Act); a scenario describing an agreement obtained by threatening a party tests free consent (the agreement is voidable at the coerced party's option, due to coercion vitiating consent); a scenario describing an informal promise with nothing exchanged in return tests consideration (generally unenforceable without consideration, subject to specific limited exceptions). Recognizing which specific contract-law element a given scenario is testing — rather than trying to recall an entire case-law framework — is the practical Foundation-level exam skill this material builds toward.

## Why business law matters beyond exam scoring

Foundation-level business law isn't purely an academic hurdle — CA, CS, and CMA professionals all regularly encounter contract and company-law questions in practice (auditors assessing contract validity in financial statements, company secretaries handling corporate governance and compliance matters, cost accountants working within company law's financial reporting framework), making this Foundation-level legal literacy a genuine professional prerequisite, not just an exam-clearing formality.
