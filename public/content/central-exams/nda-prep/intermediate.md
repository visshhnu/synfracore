# NDA Preparation — Intermediate

## Applying topic-wise weightage to Mathematics preparation

Fundamentals lists Mathematics' full topic span; Intermediate applies practical weightage strategy: Algebra and Trigonometry together typically account for a substantial share of NDA Mathematics questions across recent years' patterns, making them high-priority topics for depth of preparation, while Vectors and Statistics/Probability, though tested, generally carry comparatively fewer questions — this doesn't mean skipping lower-weight topics, but does mean allocating proportionally more practice time to Algebra/Trigonometry mastery before extensively deepening lower-weight areas. `(needs verification — recheck against current source: exact topic-wise question distribution varies by exam cycle and should be confirmed against recent actual papers, not assumed static.)`

## Working through PYQs (previous year questions) effectively

NDA preparation benefits significantly from systematic PYQ practice — not just for content familiarity, but for recognizing NDA's specific question *style*, which tends toward direct application of formulas and concepts rather than the more elaborate multi-step problems found in some other competitive exams. Working through PYQs topic-wise (all algebra PYQs together, then all trigonometry PYQs, etc.) rather than exam-wise (one full paper at a time) is generally more effective during the learning phase, since it reveals a topic's specific question patterns more clearly; full timed papers become more valuable later in preparation, once topic-wise foundations are solid, for building exam-condition stamina and time management.

## GAT English — comprehension strategy

Applying Fundamentals' English component in practice: NDA comprehension passages are typically shorter and more directly factual than passages in some other competitive exams, rewarding a strategy of reading the passage once carefully rather than skimming and relying heavily on re-reading — since passage length doesn't usually justify the time cost of multiple full re-reads under exam time pressure. Grammar and vocabulary questions benefit from consistent exposure (reading varied English-language material regularly) more than from isolated grammar-rule memorization, since NDA's grammar questions tend to test practical usage recognition over abstract rule recitation.

## GAT General Knowledge — building sustained awareness

Applying Fundamentals' point about GK requiring ongoing habit rather than cramming: an effective approach combines a foundational science/history/geography review (covering core Class IX-X level content systematically, since this forms a stable, non-time-sensitive knowledge base) with regular current-affairs tracking closer to the exam date (since current affairs content is inherently time-sensitive and can't be front-loaded months in advance the way foundational content can). Treating these as two genuinely different preparation tracks — stable foundational knowledge built early, current affairs built continuously and reviewed intensively near the exam — is more effective than treating GK preparation as a single undifferentiated block.

## Balancing preparation time across sections given the 300/600 weight split

A candidate should recognize that GAT's 600-mark weight (English + GK combined) is double Mathematics' 300-mark weight in raw marks, but this doesn't necessarily mean doubling preparation time on GAT — Mathematics tends to have a more learnable, practice-driven skill ceiling (consistent practice reliably improves Mathematics scores), while GK's breadth means additional study time faces somewhat diminishing returns past a certain point of foundational coverage. Balancing preparation time should weigh not just raw marks but each section's actual responsiveness to additional preparation effort.
