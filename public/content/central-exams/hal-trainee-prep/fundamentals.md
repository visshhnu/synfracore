## HAL & Aerospace-Defence Static GK — the part generic GA prep misses

Because HAL is itself a defence-aerospace PSU, its GA section (and its interview) reliably draws from HAL's own corporate facts and India's aerospace/defence landscape — this is the one static-GK slice generic banking/SSC GA prep won't cover.

### HAL corporate facts (verified against Wikipedia and HAL's own notification letterheads)

- **Founded:** 23 December 1940, by industrialist Walchand Hirachand — originally as Hindustan Aircraft Limited
- **Headquarters:** Bengaluru, Karnataka
- **Administrative control:** Ministry of Defence, Government of India
- **CPSE category:** Maharatna — conferred October 2024, making HAL the 14th Indian central public sector enterprise to hold Maharatna status (the highest CPSE category, above Navratna and Miniratna)
- **Sector:** Aerospace and defence manufacturing — military aircraft, helicopters, aero-engines, avionics

### Key HAL products (a recurring GA/interview theme)

| Product | Type |
|---|---|
| Tejas (LCA) | Light Combat Aircraft — India's first indigenous fighter jet |
| Su-30MKI | Multirole fighter, license-built for the Indian Air Force |
| Dhruv (ALH) | Advanced Light Helicopter |
| Prachand (LCH) | Light Combat Helicopter |
| Chetak / Cheetah | Legacy light utility helicopters |

### The Maharatna/Navratna/Miniratna hierarchy — a common confusion point

Indian CPSEs are graded into three tiers based on financial performance and operational independence: **Maharatna** (highest — greatest financial and operational autonomy), **Navratna** (mid tier), and **Miniratna** (entry tier, itself split into Category I and II). A CPSE typically moves up this ladder over time as it meets stricter revenue/profit/global-presence criteria — HAL's move to Maharatna in October 2024 is itself a live example of that progression, and a natural GA question anchor. `(needs verification — recheck current Maharatna CPSE count and list before relying on it, since new CPSEs are periodically added)`

## Reading GA prep the way this exam actually rewards it

Static GK doesn't change week to week, so it's the highest-return-per-hour category to lock in early and revise, not re-learn, close to exam day. Current affairs, by contrast, has a shelf life — the closer to the exam a candidate reviews the last 6 months of national/international news, science & tech milestones, and government schemes, the more of it will still be fresh on exam day. Sequencing matters more than total hours here: static GK early, current affairs last.
