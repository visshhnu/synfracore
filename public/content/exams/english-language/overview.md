# English Language — Banking & SSC Exams

## Why This Section Exists Separately

English Language is the section where non-native-medium candidates lose the most avoidable marks — not because the content is hard, but because it's rarely given dedicated daily practice compared to Quant and Reasoning. Treating it as a skill to drill, not just "read more," closes that gap fastest.

## Core Topic Map

| Topic | What's tested | Weightage (typical Prelims) |
|---|---|---|
| Reading Comprehension | 1–2 passages, inference + vocabulary-in-context questions | 7–10 questions |

## Grammar Rules That Cover Most Error-Spotting Questions

- **Subject-verb agreement** with collective nouns and "each/every" constructions.
- **Tense consistency** across a sentence, especially in reported speech and conditional sentences.
- **Preposition usage** with common verb-preposition pairs ("comply with," "arrive at," "differ from").
- **Parallelism** in lists and comparisons ("not only... but also," correlative conjunctions).

## Reading Comprehension Strategy


1. Read the questions before the passage for factual/detail questions — this saves a full re-read.
2. Read the full passage once for inference and tone-based questions — these can't be shortcut by skimming.
3. For vocabulary-in-context questions, always re-substitute the option into the original sentence rather than relying on the dictionary meaning alone — exam context often shifts the intended sense.

## Prelims vs Mains Difference

Mains English typically raises difficulty in RC passages (denser, more abstract topics — economic/policy themes are common) and adds sentence-improvement and error-correction variants beyond the Prelims format, alongside stricter time pressure per question.

## Study Approach

- Read one editorial-style passage daily (business/economy focused mirrors real exam RC topics well) and summarise it in 3 sentences — this builds both vocabulary and inference speed.
- Maintain a running list of grammar rules you personally get wrong — generic grammar revision is far less efficient than a personalised error log.
