# English Language — FAQ

**I'm a strong reader in general — why do I still lose marks on error detection and phrase replacement specifically?**
General reading ability doesn't automatically build familiarity with the specific trap patterns these question types use (like a plural noun placed near the verb to tempt incorrect subject-verb agreement) or with formal-register collocations ("make a decision" vs. "take a decision") that differ from casual or regional English usage. These sections reward dedicated, pattern-specific practice — recognizing recurring trap structures and standard exam-English collocations — more than general reading volume alone.

**Is "read more" actually a good strategy for improving this section, or is more targeted practice needed?**
General reading helps over a long timeline but is slow and unreliable for exam-specific preparation windows. More targeted practice — daily editorial-style reading specifically (which mirrors real RC passage register and vocabulary), a personalized grammar error log, and vocabulary logging with context sentences — builds the specific skills these exams test far more efficiently than general reading of any content.

**Should I always trust a dictionary definition when answering vocabulary-in-context questions?**
Not in isolation — many words have multiple, sometimes near-opposite meanings depending on context or regional usage. Always re-substitute your chosen answer back into the original sentence and confirm it produces a coherent meaning in that specific context, rather than relying purely on whichever dictionary definition comes to mind first.

**Why does my grammar revision not seem to be improving my error-detection accuracy, even though I study grammar rules regularly?**
Generic grammar revision spends equal time on rules you already handle correctly and your actual, specific weak points, which is inefficient. A personalized error log — recording the SPECIFIC rule category (a specific preposition pairing, subject-verb agreement with collective nouns) each time you miss an error-spotting question, and reviewing that log's recurring patterns — concentrates revision time on your genuine gaps rather than diffusing it evenly across material you may already know.

**How is Mains-level English different enough from Prelims that I need to change my preparation approach?**
Mains RC passages are typically denser and more abstract (often economic/policy-themed), frequently presenting multiple perspectives without a single clear authorial stance, which tests characterizing perspectives and their relationships rather than locating one clear answer. Mains also often introduces sentence improvement (selecting the best phrasing among several grammatically valid options) rather than pure error detection (spotting a single clearly wrong element) — this needs exposure to varied correct phrasing through wide reading of formal prose, not just rule-based grammar study.

**Is it worth spending significant time on a genuinely difficult RC passage if I'm running short on time?**
Not necessarily — recognizing within the first couple of minutes that a specific passage is proving disproportionately time-costly, and making a deliberate choice to prioritize other, more tractable sections first before returning to it with remaining time, generally produces a better overall section score than rigid adherence to an original plan regardless of how a specific passage is actually unfolding. This is the same selective-attempting principle that applies to unusually difficult reasoning puzzles.
