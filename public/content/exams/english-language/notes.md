# English Language — Notes

## Core mental model

- English Language is where non-native-medium candidates lose the most avoidable marks, largely from lack of dedicated daily practice compared to Quant/Reasoning — treat it as a skill to drill systematically, not "read more" passively.
- Question-first RC strategy saves time on factual/detail questions specifically — it does NOT substitute for genuinely reading and understanding the full passage, which inference/tone questions require.
- A personalized grammar error log (recording specific rule categories you personally miss) is far more time-efficient than generic grammar revision spread evenly regardless of actual weak points.

## Vocabulary and comprehension technique

- Always re-substitute a vocabulary answer option back into the original sentence rather than relying on a memorized dictionary definition alone — many words (e.g., "table") have context-dependent or even near-opposite meanings by usage/region.
- Cloze test: read the FULL passage first before filling any blank — a word can be grammatically valid in isolation but wrong for the passage's established tone.
- Build vocabulary by logging unfamiliar words WITH their actual context sentence (not just word + definition), then apply spaced repetition (Day 1/3/7/21) to that log specifically.

## Sentence-level technique

- Para jumbles: find the ANCHOR sentence first (introduces the topic, no backward-referencing language), then use linking words ("however," "this," "accordingly") to chain the rest — a systematic, checkable process, not guesswork.
- A "positions 1 and 6 fixed" jumble variant is actually EASIER than a full jumble (two anchors already given), despite looking more complex with more sentences involved.
- Error detection traps follow recognizable patterns: a plural noun placed near the verb (inside a prepositional phrase, or after "each/every") to tempt incorrect agreement with the nearby noun instead of the true subject.
- Phrase replacement tests standard English COLLOCATIONS ("make a decision" vs. "take a decision"), not just grammar — a distinct skill from spotting broken sentences, closer to convention-familiarity than rule-application.
- Double-blank fill-ins test the RELATIONSHIP between the two chosen words, not just whether each independently fits its own local grammar — a distinct skill from single-blank questions.

## Mains-level shift

- Mains RC passages are denser, more abstract (economic/policy themes), often presenting multiple perspectives without a single clear authorial stance — tests accurately characterizing perspectives and their relationship, not just locating "what the author believes."
- Sentence improvement (Mains) differs from error detection (Prelims) — multiple options may be grammatically valid; the task is identifying the most precise/idiomatic phrasing, which needs exposure to varied correct phrasing, not just rule-based study.

## Time management

- Rough starting budgets: RC ~10-12 min, cloze ~5-6 min, error detection ~3-4 min (fastest once trap patterns are internalized), para jumbles ~5-6 min, phrase/fill-in ~4-5 min — personalize via mock practice.
- Recognize early (within 2-3 min) when a passage is proving unusually hard, and consider deprioritizing it in favor of more tractable sections first — same selective-attempting principle as this platform's Reasoning Ability content, applied to RC passages.

## Building the habit

- Daily editorial-style reading (business/economy focus) + 3-sentence summary builds reading speed (register-specific fluency) AND summarization skill (core-argument identification) simultaneously — general reading of unrelated content doesn't build the same register-specific fluency at similar volume.
