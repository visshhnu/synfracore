# JEE Main — FAQ

**Should I attempt more or fewer numerical-value questions if I'm running low on time?**
Since numerical-value questions carry no negative marking, they're strictly worth attempting whenever you have any genuine working method — there's no downside risk from a wrong answer. If time is genuinely limited, prioritize numerical questions you can solve fastest among the 10 available (only 5 count), and apply more conservative, partial-elimination-only judgment specifically to the negatively-marked MCQ section.

**Does a harder shift on exam day actually hurt my result?**
Not necessarily — percentile normalization compares you only against candidates in your own specific shift, correcting for shift-to-shift difficulty variance. If a shift is genuinely harder for everyone in it, that's accounted for in how percentiles are calculated for that shift. A paper feeling subjectively difficult isn't the same as your relative standing being damaged.

**Is it worth taking the January session seriously if I'm confident I'll do better in April anyway?**
Yes — treating January as a genuine attempt (not a disposable practice run) matters because your best score across both sessions counts, and a strong January result removes real pressure from April, letting April genuinely function as an improvement attempt rather than a must-succeed one. Under-preparing for January on the assumption that April will naturally be better effectively compresses your real preparation timeline rather than giving you two genuine opportunities.

**Should I skip NCERT and go straight to advanced problem sets since I want to prepare for JEE Advanced eventually?**
Not for Main preparation specifically — JEE Main's more formula-direct question style means a substantial share of questions are directly answerable from genuine NCERT-level mastery, disproportionately more than in Advanced. Rushing past NCERT to reach advanced material sooner is a common way to leave accessible, high-confidence marks unclaimed in exactly the type of question that should be a candidate's most reliable scoring area for Main specifically — advanced-level depth becomes more essential once actually preparing for Advanced itself.

**How should I decide what to focus on between the January and April sessions?**
Compare your January result against your own established mock-test performance trend, not in isolation. If you underperformed relative to your mocks, the likely cause is an exam-day execution issue (nerves, time management) best addressed with more full-condition mock practice. If your January result was actually consistent with your mock trend, the gap is more likely a genuine content weakness, and targeted revision of specific weak topics (identified through systematic wrong-answer analysis) is the better use of preparation time.

**If I qualify for JEE Advanced, can I keep using the same preparation strategy that worked for Main?**
Not without meaningful adjustment — Main rewards fast, direct formula recall, while Advanced questions are more conceptual and often require combining multiple concepts in a single problem, a genuinely different skill. Continuing Main-style formula-drilling practice without shifting toward harder, multi-concept problem sets during Advanced preparation often means speed built for Main doesn't transfer cleanly, which matters given the typically short preparation window between Main results and the Advanced exam.
