## 2. Key thinkers and theories, by name

| Thinker | School / Association | One-line core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Plato | Classical political philosophy | The ideal state is ruled by philosopher-kings guided by reason, not popular opinion |
| Aristotle | Classical political philosophy | Man is a political animal; the best constitution mixes elements to avoid the extremes of oligarchy/democracy |
| Machiavelli | Realist statecraft | A ruler should be judged by effective results, not conventional morality ("The Prince") |
| Thomas Hobbes | Social contract | Without a strong sovereign, life in the state of nature is a war of all against all |
| John Locke | Social contract, Liberalism | Government exists to protect natural rights (life, liberty, property); consent-based legitimacy |
| John Stuart Mill | Liberalism, Utilitarianism | Individual liberty should be limited only to prevent harm to others |
| Karl Marx | Marxism | The state is an instrument of class domination; history moves through class struggle |
| Antonio Gramsci | Neo-Marxism | Ruling-class domination operates through cultural hegemony, not just coercion |
| Hannah Arendt | 20th-century political theory | Power arises from people acting in concert, distinct from violence or coercion |
| John Rawls | Liberal theory of justice | Justice as fairness: rules chosen behind a "veil of ignorance" protect the worst-off (difference principle) |
| M. K. Gandhi | Gandhism | Truth and non-violence (Satyagraha) as both political method and moral end |
| B. R. Ambedkar | Dalit political thought, constitutionalism | Social democracy must precede political democracy; annihilation of caste as a political project |
| M. N. Roy | Radical Humanism | Moved from orthodox Marxism to a humanist, individual-reason-centred radicalism |
| Sri Aurobindo | Indian nationalist/spiritual thought | Spiritual nationalism; India's freedom as part of a larger spiritual evolution |
| Sir Syed Ahmed Khan | Muslim modernist reform thought | Modern education and political caution for Muslims under British rule |

## 3. IR approaches, at a glance

| Approach | Core assumption |
|---|---|
| Idealism | International cooperation, law, and institutions can and should overcome power politics |
| Realism | States are self-interested, rational actors in an anarchic system; power and security dominate |
| Marxism (IR) | Global relations reflect capitalist economic structures and class/core-periphery exploitation |
| Functionalism | Technical/economic cooperation between states spills over into wider political cooperation |
| Systems theory | The international system as a whole (its structure and interactions) shapes state behaviour, not just individual state choices |

## 4. Key institutions, groupings, and terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| NAM (Non-Aligned Movement) | Grouping of states declining formal alignment with either Cold War bloc; India a founding member |
| SAARC | South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation — India, Pakistan, and other South Asian states |
| NIEO | New International Economic Order — Third World demand for a fairer global economic system, raised at the UN in the 1970s |
| CMEA | Council for Mutual Economic Assistance — Soviet-bloc economic cooperation organisation |
| Bretton Woods system | Post-WWII international monetary order that created the IMF and World Bank |
| Basic Structure doctrine | Judicial doctrine (from *Kesavananda Bharati*, 1973) holding that Parliament cannot amend the Constitution's core/basic features |
| Veil of ignorance | Rawls' thought experiment: designing justice principles without knowing your own position in society |
| Hegemony (Gramscian) | Domination achieved through cultural/ideological leadership, not just coercion |
| Balance of power | States act to prevent any single state from becoming overwhelmingly dominant |
| Collective security | An attack on one state is treated as an attack on all, deterring aggression collectively (the UN's founding logic) |

## 5. Revision strategy

**2-3 months before exam:**
- Finish first read-through of both papers; complete the thinker one-pagers (Paper I, Units 9-10) and the IR-approaches comparison table (Paper II, Unit 5).

**1 month before:**
- Solve the last 10 years of PYQs for both papers under timed conditions.
- Refresh the current-affairs-to-theory bridge notes (Paper II Section B) weekly, since this is the fastest-changing part of the syllabus.

**1 week before:**
- Only previous year papers and your own condensed notes — no new sources.
- Time at least one full mock paper per section.

**1 day before:**
- Light revision only: thinker one-pagers, the IR-approaches table, and the theories-of-state comparison — no new topics.
