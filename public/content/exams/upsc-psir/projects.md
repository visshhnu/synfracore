# UPSC PSIR Optional — Practice Sets

These are practice **prompts**, not model answers — the point is to build your own analytical answer-writing reflexes under realistic conditions. Full model answers with examiner-level marking are handled by a separate premium feature; this section gives you the structured exercises to practice against.

## Practice Set 1: Thinker & Concept Flashcard Drills

**Level:** Beginner | **Time:** 10-15 minutes per drill, daily

Paper I Units 9-10 name specific thinkers, and Units 2-8 name specific theoretical positions — both are the highest-guaranteed-return memorisation work in the entire optional, because they recur across both Section A and Section B answers.

### Drill instructions
1. Pick one thinker or concept per session from the rotation below.
2. Without notes, write from memory: the core idea in one sentence, the associated theory/ideology, one specific text or concept, and one standard criticism.
3. Check against your notes and mark any gaps for the next session.

### Rotation
- **Western political thought:** Plato, Aristotle, Machiavelli, Hobbes, Locke, John S. Mill, Marx, Gramsci, Hannah Arendt.
- **Indian political thought:** Dharmashastra/Arthashastra/Buddhist traditions, Sir Syed Ahmed Khan, Sri Aurobindo, M. K. Gandhi, B. R. Ambedkar, M. N. Roy.
- **Theories of state:** Liberal, Neo-liberal, Marxist, Pluralist, post-colonial, Feminist.
- **IR approaches:** Idealist, Realist, Marxist, Functionalist, Systems theory.
- **Named models/theorists in comparative politics and IR:** balance of power, deterrence, collective security, hegemonic stability (as applicable to Unit 6/7 concepts).

### Skill demonstrated
Rapid, structured recall of named theorists and schools — the raw material every Paper I and Paper II answer eventually draws on.

## Practice Set 2: Answer-Writing Practice — Paper I (Political Theory and Indian Politics)

**Level:** Intermediate | **Time:** 20-25 minutes per question, simulate real exam timing

Work through these UPSC-style prompts one at a time. Write a full structured answer (introduction → body with 2-4 dimensions → critical angle → conclusion) within the time limit implied by the marks.

### Sample prompts (practice these — do not just read them)
1. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Examine Rawls' theory of justice. How valid is the communitarian critique of his 'veil of ignorance'?" (Section A, Unit 3: Justice)
2. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Distinguish between representative, participatory, and deliberative models of democracy. Which model best addresses the deficits of the others?" (Section A, Unit 6: Democracy)
3. *(10 marks / ~15 min)* "Explain Gramsci's concept of hegemony and illustrate it with one historical example." (Section A, Unit 7: Concept of Power)
4. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Critically examine the Marxist theory of state. To what extent does it explain the modern welfare state?" (Section A, Unit 2)
5. *(20 marks / ~25 min)* "Discuss the salient features of the Indian Constitution's Basic Structure doctrine and evaluate its impact on Parliament's amending power." (Section B, Unit 3)
6. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Examine the significance of the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments for grassroots democracy in India." (Section B, Unit 5)
7. *(20 marks / ~25 min)* "Account for the changing nature of centre-state relations in Indian federalism, citing at least one recent inter-state dispute." (Section B, Unit 7)

### After each attempt, self-check against
- Did the introduction define the key term/doctrine in one or two lines, without padding?
- Did the body cover at least 2-3 distinct dimensions (not just one long list of facts)?
- Is there at least one genuine critical angle — a limitation, a counter-view, or a real-world complication?
- Does the conclusion offer a reasoned judgment rather than just repeating the introduction?

## Practice Set 3: Answer-Writing Practice — Paper II (Comparative Politics and IR)

**Level:** Intermediate/Advanced | **Time:** 20-25 minutes per question

### Sample prompts
1. *(15 marks)* "Compare the Realist and Idealist approaches to International Relations. Which better explains the persistence of the UN Security Council's veto system?" (Section A, Unit 5)
2. *(15 marks)* "Examine the changing nature of the state under globalisation in developed and developing societies." (Section A, Units 2 and 4)
3. *(20 marks)* "Discuss the evolution of the international economic system from Bretton Woods to the WTO. How has the Third World's demand for a New International Economic Order fared?" (Section A, Unit 8)
4. *(15 marks)* "Evaluate the case for and against UN reform, with specific reference to Security Council expansion." (Section A, Unit 9 / Section B, Unit 6)
5. *(10 marks)* "Account for the persistent impediments to SAARC's effectiveness as a regional grouping." (Section B, Unit 3)
6. *(15 marks)* "Discuss the evolution of India's Non-Alignment policy from the Cold War era to the present concept of 'strategic autonomy.'" (Section B, Unit 2)
7. *(20 marks)* "Critically examine India's changing nuclear policy since 1998, and its implications for India's global standing." (Section B, Unit 7)

## Practice Set 4: Current-Affairs-to-Theory Bridge Building (Capstone)

**Level:** Advanced | **Time:** Ongoing, one current event per week

This is the highest-leverage practice exercise for Paper II Section B specifically, because the syllabus's own "recent developments" examples are dated (see the Advanced section's flagged note) — you must actively supply current material yourself, and this exercise builds that habit.

### Steps
1. Choose one current international-relations or Indian-foreign-policy news item per week (a summit, a bilateral visit, a UN vote, a regional dispute development).
2. For that event, write a one-page bridge note: which Section A concept/theory it illustrates (e.g., balance of power, collective security, a specific IR approach), which Section B unit it belongs to (bilateral relation, regional cooperation, nuclear policy, etc.), and one line on how it could be used as a contemporary example in an answer.
3. Cross-reference back to the relevant Paper I concept where relevant (e.g., a domestic political reaction to a foreign-policy decision links back to Section B's party system or social movements units).
4. After building 8-10 such bridge notes, attempt a comparative question (e.g., "Compare India's approach to two different global centres of power over the last five years") using only your own notes — no external lookups — to test how usable your notes actually are under exam conditions.

### Skill demonstrated
Turning current affairs into theory-grounded, exam-ready examples — the single skill that most separates a PSIR answer that feels current from one that reads as if frozen at the syllabus's drafting date.

## Practice checklist
- [ ] Completed at least 4 weeks of daily thinker/concept flashcard drills across all five rotation categories
- [ ] Written full timed answers for at least 10 Paper I prompts and 10 Paper II prompts
- [ ] Built at least 8 current-affairs-to-theory bridge notes
- [ ] Attempted at least one full-length, full-timing mock paper (both Paper I and Paper II)
- [ ] Self-reviewed every answer against the four-question checklist above
