# NEET Mock Tests — Notes

## Core mental model

- 30+ full mocks correlates with meaningfully higher scores than subject-study alone — the exam-taking skill (time management, pacing, negative-marking strategy) is trainable and distinct from subject knowledge.
- Mock tests only have real value if taken under genuine exam conditions (full 3-hour window, no pausing, no phone, OMR bubbling practice) — casual practice doesn't train the actual skill the exam requires.
- The most-skipped step (analyzing every wrong answer: topic, why wrong, what to revise) is where almost all of a mock's real diagnostic value lives — taking the mock without this analysis wastes most of its usefulness.

## Time and question strategy

- Starting time allocation: Biology ~50 min, Physics ~65 min, Chemistry ~65 min — a starting framework to personalize via actual mock performance, not a rigid rule.
- Two-pass strategy: pass 1 attempts only fast, confident questions; pass 2 returns to harder ones with remaining time — protects against losing easy marks by getting stuck early on hard questions.
- Subject-attempt order should be decided once and practiced consistently across mocks, not changed exam-day based on how the paper "feels."

## Negative marking strategy

- Blind guessing (0 options eliminated) is technically positive expected value (+0.25) but high-variance — the better strategy is attempting only when at least 1-2 options can be eliminated (EV rises to +1.5 at 50% confidence), and leaving genuinely unknown questions blank.
- This requires honest real-time self-assessment during the exam ("do I have partial knowledge or am I guessing blind"), not just knowing the scoring formula.

## Diagnosing weak points

- Rank weak topics by total MARKS LOST (frequency × mark value), not just raw wrong-answer count — a frequent, high-value topic with moderate errors can cost more than a rare topic with high errors.
- Apply spaced repetition specifically to mock-identified mistakes (Day 1/3/7/21 review) — more targeted and higher-leverage than general content review, since it directly addresses confirmed gaps.
- Track accuracy and attempt rate SEPARATELY, not just overall score — low attempt rate + high accuracy signals a time-management problem; high attempt rate + low accuracy signals a knowledge/confidence problem. Each needs a different fix.

## Score plateaus

- Three distinct causes: (1) weak-topic analysis identified but revision follow-through isn't happening, (2) a genuine content-depth ceiling requiring harder practice material, (3) a time-management problem being mistaken for a knowledge gap. Misdiagnosing which one is active wastes preparation time on the wrong fix.

## Fatigue, anxiety, and the final week

- Mental fatigue accumulates within a single day of back-to-back mocks — later-mock scores that day can be artificially depressed, not a true reflection of knowledge. Space mocks appropriately (generally ≤1 full mock/day).
- Genuine test anxiety (persistent physical symptoms, blanking on known material) is a real, addressable issue distinct from normal nervousness — not something to push through with more raw study hours alone.
- Final week shifts strategy deliberately: fewer/no new full mocks, review the accumulated mistake log, protect sleep — this is a strategic consolidation, not a slowdown.
