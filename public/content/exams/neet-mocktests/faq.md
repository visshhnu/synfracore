# NEET Mock Tests — FAQ

**How many mocks should I actually take before the real exam?**
The Overview tab's guidance (30+ full mocks correlating with a meaningfully higher score) is a reasonable target, but the phased schedule matters more than the raw count — chapter-wise tests early, subject-wise in the middle phase, and full 3-hour mocks concentrated in the final month, tapering off in the final week. Taking 30 full mocks all crammed into the last two weeks isn't equivalent to the same number spread across the recommended phased schedule.

**Should I always guess if I'm unsure, since not attempting gives zero marks anyway?**
No — this misunderstands the actual expected-value math. A completely blind guess (no options eliminated) has a small positive expected value but real, risky variance; the better strategy is attempting only when you can eliminate at least 1-2 options through genuine partial knowledge, and leaving truly unknown questions blank. Guessing blind on every uncertain question, rather than being selective, tends to produce a worse actual outcome across a full paper than a more disciplined, partial-knowledge-only attempt strategy.

**Is it better to attempt questions in the order they appear, or skip around?**
Skipping around (via a two-pass strategy — fast, confident questions first, harder ones in a second pass with remaining time) generally outperforms strict paper-order attempts. Answering in strict order risks getting stuck on a difficult early question and running out of time for several easy, fast questions later in the paper — pure lost marks that a two-pass approach specifically protects against.

**I keep getting the same topics wrong across multiple mocks even though I've reviewed them. What am I doing wrong?**
This is one of the clearest signals of a score plateau (covered on the Advanced tab) specifically caused by a revision follow-through gap — the mistake is being correctly identified in analysis, but the actual scheduled, dedicated revision time on that specific topic isn't happening, or isn't happening with enough repetition (spaced repetition specifically applied to that mistake, not just a single review) to actually fix it. Simply noting a weak topic isn't the same as actually revising it with the spacing needed for retention.

**Should I take multiple full 3-hour mocks in the same day to maximize practice?**
Generally not recommended — mental fatigue accumulates within a single day of back-to-back mocks, and a mock taken third or fourth in one day often produces an artificially depressed score reflecting fatigue rather than true knowledge gaps. This can mislead your analysis, making a topic look weaker than it actually is simply because it was tested while fatigued. Spacing mocks appropriately (generally no more than one full mock per day) produces more reliable, genuinely diagnostic data.

**What should my strategy be in the final week before the exam?**
Shift from "maximize new practice" to "consolidate and protect" — reduce full mock frequency significantly (1-2 at most, not daily), focus review time on your accumulated mistake log from prior months rather than new content, and deliberately protect sleep, since sleep directly affects memory consolidation and exam-day cognitive performance. This is a deliberate strategic change specific to the final week, not a sign of reduced effort or motivation.
