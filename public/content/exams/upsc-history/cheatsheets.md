# UPSC History Optional — Quick Reference Cheatsheets

## 1. Full syllabus topic list, by paper and unit

### Paper I — Ancient and Medieval Indian History (24 units)
| Unit | Core topics |
|---|---|
| 1. Sources | Archaeological (exploration, excavation, epigraphy, numismatics, monuments), literary (indigenous, poetry, scientific, regional, religious), foreign accounts (Greek, Chinese, Arab) |
| 2. Pre-history and Proto-history | Geographical factors, Paleolithic/Mesolithic hunting-gathering, Neolithic/Chalcolithic agriculture |
| 3. Indus Valley Civilization | Origin, date, extent, characteristics, decline, survival, significance, art and architecture |
| 4. Megalithic Cultures | Pastoral/farming culture distribution, community life, settlements, agriculture, crafts, iron industry |
| 5. Aryans and Vedic Period | Aryan expansion, Rig Vedic to later Vedic transformation, political/social/economic life, monarchy and Varna system |
| 6. Period of Mahajanapadas | State formation, urban centres, trade/coinage, Jainism/Buddhism, rise of Magadha and Nandas, foreign invasions |
| 7. Mauryan Empire | Chandragupta/Kautilya, Ashoka's Dhamma/edicts, polity, art, disintegration, Sungas/Kanvas |
| 8. Post-Mauryan Period | Indo-Greeks, Sakas, Kushanas, Western Kshatrapas; outside-world contact; Mahayana; art and culture |
| 9. Early State/Society — East India, Deccan, South India | Kharavela, Satavahanas, Tamil Sangam states; land grants, trade guilds; Sangam literature |
| 10. Guptas, Vakatakas, Vardhanas | Polity, coinage, Indian feudalism, caste, Nalanda/Vikramshila/Vallabhi, literature and art |
| 11. Regional States during Gupta Era | Kadambas, Pallavas, Chalukyas, Palas, Senas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas, Hoysalas, Pandyas; temple institutions |
| 12. Themes in Early Indian Cultural History | Languages/texts, art and architecture evolution, philosophy, science and mathematics |
| 13. Early Medieval India, 750-1200 | Rajput origins, Cholas, Indian feudalism, agrarian economy, caste proliferation, women |
| 14. Cultural Traditions in India, 750-1200 | Shankaracharya/Ramanuja/Madhva, Bhakti, arrival of Islam and Sufism, Rajtarangini, temple architecture |
| 15. The Thirteenth Century | Ghurian invasions, Delhi Sultanate establishment, Iltutmish, Balban |
| 16. The Fourteenth Century | Khalji Revolution, Alauddin Khalji, Muhammad Tughluq, Firuz Tughluq, sultanate decline, Ibn Battuta |
| 17. Society, Culture, Economy — 13th/14th c. | Rural/ruling-class society, caste and slavery, Bhakti/Sufi, Sultanate architecture, agricultural/urban economy |

| 20. Akbar | Conquests, jagir/mansab systems, Rajput policy, Sulh-i-kul, court patronage |

| 22. Economy and Society, 16th/17th c. | Population, trade with Europe, mercantile classes, banking, peasants, Sikh community/Khalsa Panth |
| 23. Culture during the Mughal Empire | Persian/Hindi literature, Mughal architecture and painting, classical music, science |
| 24. The Eighteenth Century | Mughal decline factors, regional principalities, Maratha ascendancy, Panipat (1761), eve of British conquest |

## 3. Key events and dates — Modern India

| Event | Date | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Battle of Plassey | 1757 | Clive defeats Siraj-ud-Daulah; opens British political control of Bengal |
| Battle of Buxar | 1764 | Confirms British supremacy over Bengal, Awadh, and the Mughal emperor |
| Regulating Act | 1773 | First British parliamentary attempt to regulate Company rule |
| Permanent Settlement | 1793 | Fixed, perpetual Bengal land revenue under Cornwallis |
| Charter Act | 1833 | Ends Company's trade monopoly; centralises legislative authority |
| Revolt of 1857 | 1857 | Widest anti-colonial uprising before the 20th century; ends Company rule (1858) |
| Indian National Congress founded | 1885 | Start of organised, associational Indian politics |
| Partition of Bengal / Swadeshi Movement | 1905 | Triggers organised boycott politics and the moderate-extremist split |
| Rowlatt Satyagraha | 1919 | Gandhi's first all-India mass campaign |
| Non-Cooperation Movement | 1920-22 | First mass nationalist movement under Gandhi; withdrawn after Chauri Chaura |
| Civil Disobedience Movement | 1930-34 | Includes the Dandi Salt March |
| Quit India Movement | 1942 | "Do or Die"; final mass movement before independence |
| Independence and Partition | 1947 | Transfer of power; creation of India and Pakistan |

## 5. Important historians and historiographical schools

| Historian/School | Association | One-line core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Colonial/Cambridge School (e.g., Anil Seal) | Colonial-era and mid-20th-century administrator/academic historiography | Indian nationalism explained largely as elite factional competition for colonial patronage |
| Nationalist historiography | Freedom-struggle-era Indian historians | The freedom movement as a genuinely popular, morally unified anti-colonial struggle |
| D.D. Kosambi | Marxist historiography (pioneer) | Ancient/early Indian history read through modes of production and social formation |
| R.S. Sharma | Marxist historiography | Indian feudalism thesis; standard reference text for ancient India |
| Irfan Habib | Marxist historiography | Agrarian economy and class relations in Mughal India |
| Ranajit Guha | Subaltern Studies (founder) | *A Rule of Property for Bengal* — the Permanent Settlement's actual impoverishing effect on Bengal's peasantry; later led the broader Subaltern Studies shift toward non-elite historical agency |
| V.D. Savarkar | Nationalist historiography | *The Indian War of Independence* (1909) — reframed 1857 as a unified war of national liberation |
| Bipan Chandra | Nationalist-modernist historiography | *India's Struggle for Independence* — standard reference for the freedom movement |
| Sumit Sarkar | Left-nationalist historiography | *Modern India 1885-1947* — nuanced account balancing elite and popular politics |

## 6. Common terminology quick-glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Epigraphy | The study of inscriptions carved on stone, metal, or other durable material |
| Numismatics | The study of coins as historical evidence of rulers, dates, trade, and economy |
| Sanskritisation-adjacent term: Varna system | The fourfold ancient Indian social classification (Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra) |
| Sulh-i-kul | Akbar's policy of "universal peace/tolerance" across religious communities |
| Permanent Settlement | The 1793 fixed, perpetual Bengal land-revenue arrangement with zamindars |
| Ryotwari settlement | Direct revenue assessment between the state and individual cultivators |
| Mahalwari settlement | Village- or estate-level (mahal) joint revenue assessment |
| De-industrialisation | The decline of traditional Indian manufacturing (e.g., textiles) under colonial economic policy |
| Drain of wealth | Dadabhai Naoroji's thesis on the one-way transfer of India's wealth to Britain under colonial rule |
| Safety-valve thesis | The claim that the British helped found the INC to channel and control rising discontent |
| Subaltern | A non-elite social group (peasants, tribals, labourers) whose historical agency Subaltern Studies foregrounds |
| Total war | A conflict mobilising a society's entire economic, industrial, and civilian capacity, not just its military |
| Non-alignment | The Cold War-era foreign-policy stance of not formally aligning with either major power bloc |

## 7. Revision strategy

**3-4 months before exam:**
- Finish first read-through of both papers; complete the four-column dynasty tables (Paper I) and the source/date/historian tables for the high-yield Units 7-9 and World Wars sections (Paper II).

**1-2 months before:**
- Solve the last 10 years of PYQs for both papers under timed conditions.
- Redraw your dynasty and historiography-contrast tables from memory weekly.

**2-3 weeks before:**
- Only previous year papers and your own condensed notes — no new sources.
- Time at least one full mock paper per section, split across Paper I, Modern India, and World History.

**1 day before:**
- Light revision only: your dynasty tables, historiography-contrast tables, and core dates/terminology glossary — no new topics.
