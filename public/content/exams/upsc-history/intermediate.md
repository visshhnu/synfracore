# UPSC History Optional — Intermediate (Paper I: Ancient and Medieval Indian History)

Paper I is a single continuous chronological sequence of 24 units — from the earliest archaeological sources through the eighteenth century, ending on the eve of the British conquest. This is the exact structure and topic list as given in UPSC's official syllabus.

## The full Paper I syllabus

```
1. SOURCES:
   Archaeological sources: Exploration, excavation, epigraphy, numismatics, monuments
   Literary sources: Indigenous — primary and secondary; poetry, scientific literature,
     literature in regional languages, religious literature
   Foreign accounts: Greek, Chinese and Arab writers

2. PRE-HISTORY AND PROTO-HISTORY:
   Geographical factors
   Hunting and gathering (Paleolithic and Mesolithic)
   Beginning of agriculture (Neolithic and Chalcolithic)

3. INDUS VALLEY CIVILIZATION:
   Origin, date, extent, characteristics, decline, survival and significance,
     art and architecture

4. MEGALITHIC CULTURES:
   Distribution of pastoral and farming cultures outside the Indus
   Development of community life
   Settlements, agriculture, crafts, pottery, iron industry

5. ARYANS AND VEDIC PERIOD:
   Expansion of Aryans in India
   Vedic period: religious and philosophic literature
   Transformation from Rig Vedic to later Vedic period
   Political, social, and economical life
   Significance of the Vedic Age
   Evolution of monarchy and Varna system

6. PERIOD OF MAHAJANAPADAS:
   Formation of states (Mahajanapada): republics and monarchies
   Rise of urban centres
   Trade routes; economic growth; coinage
   Spread of Jainism and Buddhism
   Rise of Magadha and the Nandas
   Iranian and Macedonian invasions and their impact

7. MAURYAN EMPIRE:
   Foundation: Chandragupta, Kautilya's Arthashastra
   Ashoka: the concept of Dharma, edicts, polity, administration, economy,
     art, architecture, sculpture, external contacts, religion, literature
   Disintegration; Sungas and Kanvas

8. POST-MAURYAN PERIOD (Indo-Greeks, Sakas, Kushanas, Western Kshatrapas):
   Contacts with the outside world
   Growth of urban centres; economy; coinage
   Development of religion (Mahayana); society
   Art, architecture, culture, literature, science

9. EARLY STATE AND SOCIETY IN EASTERN INDIA, DECCAN, AND SOUTH INDIA:
   Kharavela, Satavahanas, Tamil Sangam states
   Administration, economy: land grants, coinage, trade guilds, urban centres,
     Buddhist centres
   Sangam literature and culture, art and architecture

10. GUPTAS, VAKATAKAS AND VARDHANAS:
   Polity and administration; economic conditions
   Coinage; land grants; decline of urban centres
   Indian feudalism, caste system, position of women
   Education and institutions (Nalanda, Vikramshila, Vallabhi)
   Literature, scientific literature, art and architecture

11. REGIONAL STATES DURING GUPTA ERA:
   Kadambas, Pallavas, Chalukyas of Badami: polity, administration, trade guilds,
     literature
   Growth of Vaishnava and Saiva religions; Tamil Bhakti movement,
     Shankaracharya, Vedanta
   Temple institutions and architecture
   Palas, Senas, Rashtrakutas, Paramaras; Chalukyas of Kalyana, Cholas,
     Hoysalas, Pandyas: local government
   Growth of art and architecture; religious sects; temple institutions
   Mathas, Agraharas, education, literature, economy, society

12. THEMES IN EARLY INDIAN CULTURAL HISTORY:
   Languages and texts
   Major stages in evolution of art and architecture
   Major philosophical thinkers and schools
   Ideas in science and mathematics

13. EARLY MEDIEVAL INDIA, 750-1200:
   Polity: major political developments in North India and the Peninsula;
     origin and rise of Rajputs
   Cholas: administration, village economy, and society; Indian feudalism
   Agrarian economy and urban settlements
   Trade and commerce
   Society: status of Brahman and new social order; proliferation of castes
   Condition of women
   Development in science and technology

14. CULTURAL TRADITIONS IN INDIA, 750-1200:
   Philosophy: Shankaracharya and Vedanta, Ramanuja and Vishishtadvaita,
     Madhva and Brahma Mimansa
   Religion: forms and features of religion; Tamil devotional cult; growth
     of Bhakti; arrival of Islam and its interaction with Indian religions; Sufism
   Literature: Sanskrit literature, growth of Tamil literature, literature in
     other languages, Kalhana's Rajtarangini, Alberuni's India
   Art and architecture: temple architecture, sculpture, painting

15. THE THIRTEENTH CENTURY:
   Establishment of the Delhi Sultanate
   Ghurian invasions: factors behind Ghurian success
   Economic, social, and cultural consequences
   Early Turkish Sultans; consolidation: The rule of Iltutmish and Balban

16. THE FOURTEENTH CENTURY:
   "Khalji Revolution"
   Alauddin Khalji: conquests and territorial expansion; agrarian and economic
     measures
   Muhammad Tughluq: major projects; agrarian measures; bureaucracy
   Firuz Tughluq: agrarian measures; achievements in civil engineering and
     public works
   Causes of decline of the Sultanate
   Ibn Battuta's account

17. SOCIETY, CULTURE AND ECONOMY IN THE THIRTEENTH AND FOURTEENTH CENTURIES:
   Society: composition of rural society; ruling classes; town-dwellers; women;
     religious classes; caste and slavery under the Sultanate; the Bhakti and
     Sufi movements
   Culture: Persian literature; growth of regional languages and literature in
     North and South India; Sultanate architecture; new structural forms;
     painting; evolution of a composite culture
   Economy: agricultural production; rise of urban economy: non-agricultural
     production; trade and commerce

18. THE FIFTEENTH AND EARLY SIXTEENTH CENTURY — POLITICAL DEVELOPMENTS AND ECONOMY:
   Rise of provincial dynasties: Bengal, Kashmir (Zainul Abedin), Gujarat,
     Malwa, Bahmanids
   Vijayanagara Empire
   Lodis
   Mughal Empire: first phase (Babur and Humayun)
   Sur Empire: Sher Shah's administration
   Portuguese colonial enterprise
   Bhakti and Sufi movements

19. THE FIFTEENTH AND EARLY SIXTEENTH CENTURY — SOCIETY AND CULTURE:
   Regional cultural specificities
   Literary traditions
   Provincial architecture
   Society, culture, literature, and arts in Vijayanagara Empire

20. AKBAR:
   Conquests and consolidation of empire
   Establishment of jagir and mansab systems
   Rajput policy
   Evolution of religious and social outlook: theory of Sulh-i-kul and
     religious policy
   Court patronage of art and technology

21. MUGHAL EMPIRE IN THE SEVENTEENTH CENTURY:
   Major administrative policies of Jahangir, Shahjahan, and Aurangzeb
   The empire and the zamindars; religious policies of Aurangzeb
   Nature of the Mughal state
   Late seventeenth-century crisis; revolts
   Ahom kingdom
   Shivaji and the early Maratha Kingdom

22. ECONOMY AND SOCIETY IN THE SIXTEENTH AND SEVENTEENTH CENTURIES:
   Population; agricultural and craft production
   Towns; trade routes; commerce with Europe through the Dutch, English, and
     French companies; the "trade revolution"
   Indian mercantile classes; banking, insurance, and credit systems
   Condition of peasants; condition of women
   Evolution of the Sikh community, Khalsa Panth

23. CULTURE DURING THE MUGHAL EMPIRE:
   Persian histories and literature in other languages
   Hindi and religious literature
   Mughal architecture; Mughal painting; provincial architecture and painting
   Classical music
   Science and technology

24. THE EIGHTEENTH CENTURY:
   Factors for the decline of the Mughal Empire
   The regional principalities: Nizam's Deccan, Bengal, Awadh
   Emergence of the Maratha ascendancy under the Peshwas
   Maratha fiscal and financial system
   Emergence of Afghan Power; the Battle of Panipat (1761)
   State of politics, culture, and economy on the eve of the British conquest
```

## How the twenty-four units connect

Units 1-4 (Sources, Pre-history/Proto-history, Indus Valley, Megalithic Cultures) are the evidentiary and archaeological foundation — what evidence exists, and what the earliest urban and non-Aryan cultures looked like before the textual record proper begins. Units 5-6 (Aryans/Vedic Period, Mahajanapadas) trace the shift from tribal, pastoral society into early states and organised urban polities. Units 7-12 (Mauryas through Themes in Early Indian Cultural History) cover the first pan-Indian imperial formation and its aftermath, ending with a thematic unit that deliberately asks you to synthesise language, art, philosophy, and science across everything covered so far rather than treat each dynasty in isolation. Units 13-19 (Early Medieval India through Fifteenth/Sixteenth Century Society and Culture) trace the regional-kingdom era and the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate. Units 20-24 (Akbar through The Eighteenth Century) cover the Mughal Empire's rise, peak, and decline, ending exactly where Paper II's Unit 1 (European Penetration into India) begins.

**High-yield linkage to memorise:** Nearly every dynasty-focused unit (7, 10, 13, 15-16, 20-21) is tested along the same four dimensions — polity/administration, economy (coinage, land grants, trade), society (caste, women's status, urban life), and culture (art, architecture, literature, religion) — so building one reusable four-column note template and re-applying it to each dynasty converts 24 units of separate memorisation into one repeated structure applied 24 times.

### How to actually study the source-and-thematic units (1, 12, 17)

Units 1 (Sources), 12 (Themes in Early Indian Cultural History), and 17 (Society, Culture and Economy in the 13th-14th Centuries) are frequently under-revised because they feel abstract compared to a named-dynasty unit — but they are exactly where UPSC's synthesis and "critically examine" questions concentrate, since they explicitly ask you to compare across periods rather than describe one period in isolation. Build a running cross-reference table for these three units specifically: for any claim in Unit 12 about "evolution of art and architecture," be ready to cite the specific dynasty-level example (e.g., Gupta-era Nagara temple style vs. Chola-era Dravida style) it is drawing on.

## Cross-cutting exam skill: naming a source, date, or historiographical position in every answer

Unlike Geography, History has no compulsory map question, but it has an equivalent discipline: every strong answer names at least one specific source (an inscription, a foreign account, a text), a precise date or century, or a named historian's position — rather than narrating a sequence of events in general prose. Build this habit in Paper I first, since ancient/medieval units reward source-naming discipline (which inscription, which foreign traveller) just as heavily as Paper II's Modern India half rewards naming a specific historian's interpretation.

## Study resources
- **R.S. Sharma, "India's Ancient Past"** — standard first read for Units 1-14 (Sources through Cultural Traditions, 750-1200).
- **Upinder Singh, "A History of Ancient and Early Medieval India"** — a more recent, source-critical alternative widely used alongside or instead of Sharma.
- **Satish Chandra, "History of Medieval India"** — the standard reference for Units 15-24 (Delhi Sultanate through the Eighteenth Century).
- **Previous Year Question papers (upsc.gov.in)** — the single most reliable guide to which sub-topics within each unit are actually tested most often (Mauryan administration, Akbar's religious policy, and causes of Mughal decline are consistently high-yield).
