# UPSC History Optional — Practice Sets

These are practice **prompts**, not model answers — the point is to build your own answer-writing reflexes (naming a source, a date, or a historian, and taking a defensible position) under realistic conditions. Full model answers with examiner-level marking are handled by a separate premium feature; this section gives you the structured exercises to practice against.

## Practice Set 1: Source-and-Date Drills (Paper I)

**Level:** Beginner | **Time:** 10-15 minutes per drill

History rewards naming a specific source, inscription, or precise date, not a vague "in ancient times" framing. This drill builds that reflex directly.

### Drill instructions
1. Pick one topic from the list below.
2. In writing, name at least one archaeological source, one literary source, and one foreign account (where all three exist) that historians actually use as evidence for it.
3. Check yourself: did you name a real, specific source (a named inscription, a named text, a named traveller), or did you just write "historical records show"?

### Topics to rotate through weekly
- The extent and decline of the Indus Valley Civilization.
- Ashoka's policy of Dhamma.
- The administration of the Gupta Empire.
- Akbar's religious and social outlook.
- The causes of the decline of the Mughal Empire.

### Skill demonstrated
Fluency in source-citation, the single most consistently rewarded habit across every Paper I unit from Sources through The Eighteenth Century.

## Practice Set 2: Answer-Writing Practice — Paper I (Ancient and Medieval India)

**Level:** Intermediate | **Time:** 20-25 minutes per question, simulate real exam timing

Work through these UPSC-style prompts one at a time. Write a full structured answer (introduction defining the scope → body with 2-4 dimensions, naming at least one source/date/historian → conclusion) within the time limit implied by the marks.

### Sample prompts (practice these — do not just read them)
1. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Discuss the significance of epigraphic and numismatic evidence in reconstructing the history of the post-Mauryan period." (Unit 1/8: Sources, Post-Mauryan Period)
2. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Examine the salient features of the Varna system as it evolved during the Vedic period." (Unit 5: Aryans and Vedic Period)
3. *(20 marks / ~25 min)* "Critically examine Ashoka's concept of Dhamma. Was it a religious or a political instrument?" (Unit 7: Mauryan Empire)
4. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Discuss the nature of Indian feudalism as it developed under the Guptas and in Early Medieval India." (Unit 10/13: Guptas, Early Medieval India)
5. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Compare the agrarian measures of Alauddin Khalji and Muhammad Tughluq." (Unit 16: The Fourteenth Century)
6. *(20 marks / ~25 min)* "Assess Akbar's Rajput policy and its role in Mughal imperial consolidation." (Unit 20: Akbar)
7. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Examine the factors responsible for the decline of the Mughal Empire in the eighteenth century." (Unit 24: The Eighteenth Century)

### After each attempt, self-check against
- Did the introduction define the key term or scope in one or two lines, without padding?
- Did you name at least one specific source, date, or historian, not just narrate events in plain prose?
- Did the body cover at least 2-3 distinct dimensions (polity, economy, society, or culture), not just one long descriptive list?
- Does the conclusion add a judgment (a synthesis, a limitation, a comparative link) rather than just repeating the introduction?

## Practice Set 3: Answer-Writing Practice — Paper II (Modern India and World History)

**Level:** Intermediate/Advanced | **Time:** 20-25 minutes per question

### Sample prompts
1. *(15 marks)* "Assess the economic impact of the Permanent Settlement on Bengal's agrarian society." (Unit 4: Economic Impact of British Colonial Rule)
2. *(20 marks)* "To what extent was the Revolt of 1857 a 'national' movement? Discuss with reference to its social composition and geographical spread." (Unit 7: Indian Response to British Rule)
3. *(15 marks)* "Examine the 'safety-valve' thesis regarding the foundation of the Indian National Congress. How valid is it?" (Unit 8: Factors Leading to the Birth of Indian Nationalism)
4. *(20 marks)* "Discuss the significance of the Non-Cooperation Movement in the evolution of Gandhian mass politics." (Unit 9: Rise of Gandhi)
5. *(15 marks)* "Examine the role of linguistic reorganisation of states in India's post-independence political consolidation." (Unit 13: Consolidation as a Nation)
6. *(15 marks)* "Discuss the causes of the French Revolution and assess its impact on the development of modern political ideas." (Unit 17: Origins of Modern Politics)
7. *(20 marks)* "Critically examine the factors that led to the disintegration of the Soviet Union." (Unit 27: Disintegration of the Soviet Union and the Rise of the Unipolar World)

## Practice Set 4: Historiography-Contrast Table Building (Capstone)

**Level:** Advanced | **Time:** Ongoing, one major topic per week

This is the highest-leverage practice exercise for both papers, because a single well-built historiography-contrast table can supply citable material for multiple questions on the same topic.

### Steps
1. Choose one major topic per week that has genuine historiographical debate (1857, the causes of Mughal decline, the "safety-valve" thesis, the nature of Indian nationalism, the causes of World War I — rotate to cover both papers over your prep).
2. For that topic, build a one-page table: which named school or historian holds which position, their one-line core claim, one piece of evidence supporting it, and one criticism or counter-position.
3. Cross-reference each entry back to the specific unit and sub-topic it belongs to, so the table stays retrievable under exam conditions.
4. After building 6-8 topic tables, attempt a comparative question (e.g., "Compare the nationalist and Cambridge School interpretations of the rise of the Indian National Congress") using only your own notes — no external lookups — to test how usable your notes actually are under exam conditions.

### Skill demonstrated
Synthesis of competing historical interpretations, comparative analysis, and exam-ready recall organised by citable evidence rather than narrative description.

## Practice checklist
- [ ] Completed at least 4 weeks of source-and-date drills across Paper I topics
- [ ] Written full timed answers for at least 10 Paper I prompts and 10 Paper II prompts (split across Modern India and World History)
- [ ] Built at least 6 historiography-contrast topic tables spanning both papers
- [ ] Attempted at least one full-length, full-timing mock paper (both Paper I and Paper II)
- [ ] Self-reviewed every answer against the four-question checklist above
