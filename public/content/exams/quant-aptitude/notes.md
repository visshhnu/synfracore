# Quantitative Aptitude — Key Takeaways

- **Slow arithmetic underneath a formula, not the formula itself, is usually the real bottleneck** — number sense (divisibility rules, squares/cubes memorized cold, fast percentage-to-fraction conversion) pays off across every topic, not just the topic it's technically part of.
- **Profit/Loss percentage is always calculated on Cost Price, not Selling Price** — a specific, common, avoidable error under time pressure.
- **DI's real difficulty is reading the data correctly and fast, not the underlying calculation** — practicing DI means practicing extraction speed from tables/graphs/caselets specifically, not just arithmetic.
- **Time & Work problems become tractable once reframed as work-rate-per-day** — this single reframing replaces the need for multiple separate formulas per problem variant, including pipes-and-cisterns.
- **Number series should be checked systematically** (constant difference, ratio, second-order difference, alternating pattern, position-based) **rather than guessed at randomly** — a structured checklist is faster under time pressure than unstructured pattern-hunting.
- **Data Sufficiency questions ask whether information is enough, not for the actual answer** — solving the full problem when only sufficiency judgment was asked wastes time that should go toward other questions.
- **Compounding frequency (half-yearly/quarterly) changes both the rate and the period used in the Compound Interest formula** — using the annual rate/period directly for non-annual compounding is a specific, common error.
- **Boats and Streams is relative speed applied to a specific, recognizable pattern** — memorize the derived boat-speed/stream-speed formulas directly rather than re-deriving them from base relative-speed logic every time.
- **Speed-building requires timed, single-topic drilling, not just varied practice sets** — knowing a method and being fast enough at it under real time pressure are genuinely different skills requiring different practice modes.
- **Track accuracy and speed separately per topic** — a topic with high accuracy but slow speed still needs more drilling before test day, per Overview's own study-approach guidance.
