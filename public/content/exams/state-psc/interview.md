# State PSC — Interview / Personality Test

## What the Interview Board Evaluates

State PSC interviews (Personality Tests) evaluate administrative aptitude, awareness of state affairs, and communication — not just factual recall. Panels typically include a retired bureaucrat, an academic, and a subject expert.

## Common Question Areas

- **Your district and state**: local geography, major industries, current district collector/administrative setup, recent state government initiatives.
- **Your graduation subject and hobbies**: expect follow-up questions probing depth, not just surface familiarity.
- **Current affairs**: state-level policy decisions, recent budget allocations, ongoing welfare scheme rollouts.
- **Situational/administrative judgment**: "As a [post], how would you handle X" — testing decision-making under competing pressures (law and order vs public sentiment, resource constraints, etc).

## Preparation Approach

1. Prepare a tight 2-minute self-introduction covering education, background, and why you want to join state civil services — this is almost always the opening question.
2. Read your state's most recent budget summary and 2–3 flagship welfare schemes in detail — these come up disproportionately often.
3. Mock interviews with peers or coaching panels matter more here than solo preparation — personality tests reward comfort under real-time questioning, which is hard to simulate alone.
4. Stay updated on the district you're from and the district you studied in — panels frequently probe both.

## What Not to Do

- Don't fake confidence about a topic you don't know — panels probe follow-ups, and an honest "I don't know, but here's my reasoning" scores better than a wrong guess delivered confidently.
- Don't over-rehearse answers to the point of sounding scripted — natural, conversational responses are rated higher on communication.
