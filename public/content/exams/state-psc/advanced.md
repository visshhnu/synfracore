# State PSC — Mains Essay & Answer Writing

## Mains Structure (Typical Group I Pattern)

State PSC mains is a descriptive, written examination — usually GS papers covering History/Geography/Polity/Economy/Science & current affairs, plus a language paper (regional language + English), and in several states a dedicated Essay paper.

## Answer Writing — What Examiners Actually Reward

- **Structure over volume**: a well-structured 150-word answer with a clear intro, 2–3 points, and a conclusion scores higher than a rambling 300-word answer.
- **State-specific examples**: citing a state government scheme or a local case study where relevant signals genuine local knowledge, not just generic textbook recall.
- **Diagrams and flowcharts**: for geography and economy answers, a labelled diagram (drawn quickly, doesn't need to be elaborate) reliably picks up extra marks.
- **Keyword density**: examiners scan for syllabus keywords in the first two lines — lead with the core term the question is testing, don't bury it in the middle.

## Essay Paper Strategy

1. Pick a topic where you can sustain 3–4 distinct sub-arguments — breadth of angles matters more than depth on one point.
2. Use a simple structure: introduction (define/frame the topic) → 3–4 body paragraphs (each a distinct dimension — social, economic, political, ethical) → conclusion (forward-looking, solution-oriented).
3. Weave in state-specific context wherever the topic allows — examiners for state PSCs consistently reward locally grounded essays over generic national-level ones.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Treating mains like an expanded prelims answer (fact-dumping instead of analysis).
- Ignoring the state-specific portion of GS papers because national-level prep feels "more complete."
- Poor time allocation — running out of time on later questions because early answers were over-written.
- Illegible handwriting under time pressure — a real, frequently cited scoring cost in descriptive exams.
