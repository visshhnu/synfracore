# UPSC Geography Optional — Advanced (Paper II: Geography of India)

Paper II has ten units, all applying Paper I's principles specifically to India. UPSC's official syllabus lists these as a single continuous sequence (not split into two labelled sections the way Paper I is), though the question paper itself still uses a Section A / Section B split for the 8-question format. Candidates must additionally answer **one compulsory map-based question** pertinent to the subjects covered in this paper — this requirement is stated explicitly in the official syllabus note, not just exam convention.

## How the ten units connect

Units 1-2 (Physical Setting, Resources) are the foundation everything else stands on — you cannot explain Indian agricultural regionalisation (Unit 3) without first knowing the relief, monsoon, and soil pattern from Unit 1. Units 3-5 (Agriculture, Industry, Transport) form the "economic geography of India" cluster and are frequently asked together (e.g., "how does transport infrastructure influence industrial location in a given region"). Units 6-7 (Cultural Setting, Settlements) form the "human geography of India" cluster. Units 8-10 (Regional Development, Political Aspects, Contemporary Issues) are the applied/current-affairs-adjacent cluster, and are where daily newspaper reading (much like GS3 prep) pays off directly.

## The compulsory map question: what it actually tests

Past patterns show the map question typically asks candidates to locate and give a short note (2-3 lines each) on roughly 15-20 physical and economic features on a blank outline map of India — e.g., a specific river confluence, a mineral belt, a port, a national park, a tribal area, an industrial region `(needs verification — recheck exact feature count and marks-per-item against the current year's actual Paper II question paper, since this detail is drawn from a stable multi-year pattern observed in past papers rather than quoted from the syllabus document itself)`. The syllabus document itself only states that this question is compulsory and "pertinent to subjects covered by this paper" — it does not specify format details, so treat any specific number of features or marks split as a pattern observation from past papers, not a syllabus guarantee.

## Annotated example: a Paper II-style answer

**Question style:** "Discuss the regional pattern of Green Revolution and its socio-economic implications in India." (Unit 3, Agriculture)

1. **Introduction:** Define Green Revolution (late 1960s onward, HYV seeds + irrigation + fertilizer package) and note its geographically uneven adoption.
2. **Body — spatial pattern:** Concentrated in Punjab, Haryana, western UP (assured irrigation, flat terrain, existing canal networks) — contrast with rainfed, resource-poor regions (parts of central and eastern India) where adoption lagged.
3. **Body — socio-economic implications:** Regional income disparity (Punjab/Haryana prosperity vs. lagging eastern states), interpersonal disparity (large landholders benefited more than small/marginal farmers), and later ecological costs (groundwater depletion in Punjab, soil salinity).
4. **Diagram:** A simple India map shading the core Green Revolution belt.
5. **Conclusion:** Link to the ongoing "Second Green Revolution"/eastern-India push and crop diversification debates — a contemporary-issues (Unit 10) tie-in that strengthens the answer.

## Try it (2 minutes)

Pick any one Indian state you know reasonably well. In two minutes, list which of the ten Paper II units you could write at least one sentence about for that state (e.g., its physiographic region, its dominant crop, one industry, one settlement pattern feature). If you can touch 5+ units for a state you already know casually, that's a sign of how interconnected this syllabus really is — and how much revision leverage comes from studying states as case studies rather than units in isolation.

## Study resources
- **Majid Husain, "Geography of India"** — the standard Paper II reference text used by most serious aspirants.
- **NCERT Class 11-12 "India: Physical Environment" and "India: People and Economy"** — foundational, read before the Husain text.
- **Survey of India / school atlas** — daily map practice specifically for the compulsory map question.
- **Economic Survey (latest edition) and India Year Book** — for Unit 10 contemporary issues and Unit 4/5 current industrial/trade data.
