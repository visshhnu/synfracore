# UPSC Anthropology Optional — Quick Reference Cheatsheets

## 1. Full syllabus topic list, by paper and unit

### Paper I — Anthropology: Theory and Biology (12 units)
| Unit | Core topics |
|---|---|
| 1. Meaning, Scope and Development of Anthropology | Relationship with other disciplines, main branches (social-cultural, biological, archaeological, linguistic), human evolution theories, primate characteristics/taxonomy, hominid phylogeny (Australopithecines to Homo sapiens), cell biology/DNA, prehistoric archaeology and dating methods, cultural evolution stages (Paleolithic to Iron Age) |
| 2. Social Institutions | Nature of culture (ethnocentrism vs. cultural relativism), nature of society, marriage (types/functions/regulations), family (types/impact of urbanisation), kinship (consanguinity/affinity, descent, terminology) |
| 3. Economic Organization | Meaning/scope, Formalist-Substantivist debate, production/distribution/exchange principles |
| 4. Political Organization and Social Control | Band/tribe/chiefdom/kingdom/state, power/authority/legitimacy, social control/law/justice |
| 5. Religion | Anthropological approaches, monotheism/polytheism, sacred/profane, myths/rituals, magico-religious functionaries |
| 6. Anthropological Theories | Classical evolutionism, historical particularism, diffusionism, functionalism, structural-functionalism, structuralism, culture and personality, neo-evolutionism, cultural materialism, symbolic/interpretive theories, cognitive theories, postmodernism |
| 7. Culture, Language and Communication | Nature/origin/characteristics of language, verbal/non-verbal communication, social context of language use |
| 8. Research Methods in Anthropology | Fieldwork tradition, technique/method/methodology distinction, data collection tools, analysis/interpretation/presentation |
| 9. Human Genetics and Applied Anthropology | Genetics methods (pedigree, twin study, DNA technology), Mendelian inheritance, genetic polymorphism/Hardy-Weinberg law, chromosomal aberrations, race and racism, blood groups/genetic markers, ecological anthropology, epidemiological anthropology |
| 10. Human Growth and Development | Growth concept/stages, factors affecting growth, ageing/senescence, physique/somatotypes |
| 11. Fertility and Demography | Menarche/menopause and fertility, demographic theories, biological/socio-ecological factors in fertility/mortality |
| 12. Applications of Anthropology | Sports anthropology, nutritional anthropology, forensic anthropology, applied human genetics, DNA technology in medicine |

## 2. Key anthropological thinkers and theories

| Thinker | Associated with | One-line core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Edward Burnett Tylor | Founding definition of culture, classical evolutionism | Culture is "that complex whole" of knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom acquired as a member of society; societies pass through fixed evolutionary stages |
| Lewis Henry Morgan | Kinship classification, classical evolutionism | Systematised kinship terminology into classificatory/descriptive types via Iroquois fieldwork; proposed evolutionary stages of family forms |
| Franz Boas | Historical particularism | Each culture must be understood on its own terms and history, not ranked on a universal evolutionary scale |
| Bronisław Malinowski | Functionalism, fieldwork/participant observation | Cultural practices exist because they serve individuals' biological/psychological needs; established extended fieldwork (Trobriand Islands) as the discipline's method |
| A.R. Radcliffe-Brown | Structural-Functionalism | Cultural practices and institutions persist because they maintain the stability of the wider social structure, not just individual needs |
| Claude Lévi-Strauss | Structuralism | Underlying universal mental structures (binary oppositions) shape myths, kinship systems, and cultural phenomena across societies |
| Julian Steward / Leslie White | Neo-evolutionism | Revived evolutionary thinking in a more defensible, multilinear (Steward) or energy-technology-based (White) form |
| Marvin Harris | Cultural materialism | Material and environmental conditions (infrastructure) determine a society's social structure and ideology, not the reverse |
| Clifford Geertz | Symbolic and interpretive theories | Culture is a system of symbols and meanings; anthropology should "thickly describe" and interpret meaning, not just record behaviour |
| L.P. Vidyarthi | Sacred Complex (Indian anthropology) | Framework (from his study of Gaya) analysing a place's sacred geography, sacred performances, and sacred specialists together as one integrated complex |

## 3. Important Indian anthropological concepts and processes

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tribe-caste continuum | Many communities, especially in peninsular/central India, occupy an intermediate zone between tribe and caste rather than a hard binary |
| Sanskritization | A process (M.N. Srinivas) by which a lower caste or tribal group adopts the customs, rituals, and practices of a higher/"twice-born" caste to claim higher social status |
| Westernization | Changes introduced into Indian society through contact with Western culture, particularly under British rule (technology, institutions, values) |
| Modernization | A broader process of structural and value change associated with industrialisation, rationality, and institutional differentiation |
| Jajmani system | A traditional, hereditary system of reciprocal economic and ritual exchange between different caste groups in an Indian village |
| Sacred Complex | L.P. Vidyarthi's framework linking a place's sacred geography, sacred performances, and sacred specialists as one integrated whole |
| PVTG (Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group) | A sub-category of Scheduled Tribes identified by criteria including pre-agricultural technology, low literacy, and declining/stagnant population, warranting distinct protective attention `(needs verification — recheck current PVTG count/list against the Ministry of Tribal Affairs' latest published source)` |
| Ethno-archaeology | Studying present-day hunting, foraging, fishing, pastoral, and peasant communities to draw inferences about past (prehistoric) ways of life |
| Pseudo-tribalism | A term describing groups that adopt or claim a tribal identity or the associated legal/welfare benefits without meeting the anthropological or administrative criteria of tribal status |

## 4. Biological anthropology quick-glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hardy-Weinberg law | In an idealised population (no mutation, migration, selection, or drift), allele and genotype frequencies remain constant across generations — the baseline against which real evolutionary change is measured |
| Polygenic inheritance | A trait controlled by the combined effect of multiple genes, producing continuous variation (e.g., skin colour, height) rather than discrete categories |
| Chromosomal aberration | A numerical (e.g., an extra chromosome) or structural (e.g., a deletion, duplication, translocation) abnormality in chromosome number or arrangement |
| Genetic polymorphism | The stable occurrence of two or more distinct genetic forms (alleles) within a population at a frequency too high to be explained by mutation alone |
| Consanguinity vs. affinity | Consanguinity: relation by blood/descent. Affinity: relation through marriage |
| Descent (patrilineal/matrilineal/bilateral) | The rule by which a society traces kinship and group membership — through the father's line, the mother's line, or both |
| Somatotype | A classification of human physique/body build (commonly endomorph, mesomorph, ectomorph) used in physical anthropology's growth and constitution studies |
| Ecological anthropology | The study of bio-cultural adaptations — how human populations physiologically and culturally adapt to environmental stresses |

## 5. Constitutional and administrative provisions for tribal India

| Provision/Body | Significance |
|---|---|
| Article 342 | Empowers the President to specify Scheduled Tribes for a State/Union Territory |
| Fifth Schedule | Provides for special administration of "Scheduled Areas" and Scheduled Tribes in most states, including a Tribes Advisory Council |
| Sixth Schedule | Provides for autonomous district/regional councils administering tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram |
| Ministry of Tribal Affairs | The nodal central ministry for tribal welfare policy, PVTG programmes, and tribal development schemes `(needs verification — recheck current scheme names/scope against the Ministry's latest published material)` |
| Forest Rights Act, 2006 | Recognises forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes' and other traditional forest dwellers' rights over forest land and resources |
| National Commission for Scheduled Tribes | A constitutional body (Article 338A) monitoring safeguards for Scheduled Tribes |

## 6. Revision strategy

**3-4 months before exam:**
- Finish first read-through of both papers; complete the thinker-theory index table (Paper I) and the concept/community index table (Paper II) for the high-yield units (Anthropological Theories, Human Genetics, The Indian Social System, Tribal Situation).

**1-2 months before:**
- Solve the last 10 years of PYQs for both papers under timed conditions.
- Redraw your kinship charts, evolutionary trees, and genetic-cross diagrams from memory weekly.

**2-3 weeks before:**
- Only previous year papers and your own condensed notes — no new sources.
- Time at least one full mock paper per section, split across Paper I and Paper II.

**1 day before:**
- Light revision only: your thinker-theory index table, concept/community index table, and core terminology glossary — no new topics.
