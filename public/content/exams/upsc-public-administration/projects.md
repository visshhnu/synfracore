# UPSC Public Administration Optional — Practice Sets

These are practice **prompts**, not model answers — the point is to build your own answer-writing reflexes (naming a theorist, a constitutional provision, or a committee, and taking a defensible position) under realistic conditions. Full model answers with examiner-level marking are handled by a separate premium feature; this section gives you the structured exercises to practice against.

## Practice Set 1: Theorist-and-Model Drills (Paper I)

**Level:** Beginner | **Time:** 10-15 minutes per drill

Public Administration rewards naming a specific theorist, model, or named mechanism, not a vague "administration theory says" framing. This drill builds that reflex directly.

### Drill instructions
1. Pick one topic from the list below.
2. In writing, name at least one specific theorist or named model relevant to it, and one specific critique or limitation of that theorist's/model's view.
3. Check yourself: did you name a real, specific theorist and a real critique, or did you just write "scholars believe"?

### Topics to rotate through weekly
- Wilson's politics-administration dichotomy.
- Weber's bureaucratic model and its post-Weberian critique.
- Simon's decision-making theory versus the classical "principles of administration."
- Motivation theory (McGregor's Theory X/Y, or Herzberg/Maslow if you have studied them as contemporary theories).
- Riggs' ecological models of comparative public administration.

### Skill demonstrated
Fluency in theorist-citation, the single most consistently rewarded habit across every Paper I unit from Introduction through Financial Administration.

## Practice Set 2: Answer-Writing Practice — Paper I (Administration Theory)

**Level:** Intermediate | **Time:** 20-25 minutes per question, simulate real exam timing

Work through these UPSC-style prompts one at a time. Write a full structured answer (introduction defining the scope → body with 2-4 dimensions, naming at least one theorist/model → conclusion) within the time limit implied by the marks.

### Sample prompts (practice these — do not just read them)
1. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Discuss Woodrow Wilson's contribution to the emergence of Public Administration as a distinct discipline. How valid is the politics-administration dichotomy today?" (Unit 1: Introduction)
2. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Examine the salient features of Max Weber's bureaucratic model. Discuss its major post-Weberian critiques." (Unit 2: Administrative Thought)
3. *(20 marks / ~25 min)* "Critically examine Herbert Simon's contribution to decision-making theory in administration." (Unit 3: Administrative Behaviour)
4. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Distinguish between the systems approach and the contingency approach to organisation theory." (Unit 4: Organisations)
5. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Discuss the role of Right to Information and social audit as tools of administrative accountability." (Unit 5: Accountability and Control)
6. *(20 marks / ~25 min)* "Critically evaluate the relevance of Riggsian ecological models to the study of administration in developing societies." (Unit 7: Comparative Public Administration)
7. *(15 marks / ~20 min)* "Examine the 'anti-development thesis'. Does development administration necessarily serve development?" (Unit 8: Development Dynamics)

### After each attempt, self-check against
- Did the introduction define the key term or scope in one or two lines, without padding?
- Did you name at least one specific theorist, model, or mechanism, not just describe a concept in plain prose?
- Did the body cover at least 2-3 distinct dimensions or perspectives, not just one long descriptive list?
- Does the conclusion add a judgment (a synthesis, a limitation, an applied Indian example) rather than just repeating the introduction?

## Practice Set 3: Answer-Writing Practice — Paper II (Indian Administration)

**Level:** Intermediate/Advanced | **Time:** 20-25 minutes per question

### Sample prompts
1. *(15 marks)* "Trace the evolution of district administration in India from the colonial period to the present." (Unit 1/7: Evolution of Indian Administration, District Administration)
2. *(20 marks)* "Critically examine the changing role of the District Collector in independent India." (Unit 7: District Administration since Independence)
3. *(15 marks)* "Discuss the debate between civil service neutrality and civil service activism in the Indian context." (Unit 8: Civil Services)
4. *(20 marks)* "Assess the role of the Finance Commission in Centre-State fiscal relations in India." (Unit 6: State Government and Administration)
5. *(15 marks)* "Examine the impact of the 73rd Constitutional Amendment on rural local self-government in India." (Unit 11: Rural Development)
6. *(15 marks)* "Discuss the major recommendations of the Second Administrative Reforms Commission relevant to civil service reform." (Unit 10: Administrative Reforms since Independence)
7. *(20 marks)* "Critically examine the problems of police reform in India in the context of the National Police Commission's recommendations." (Unit 13: Law and Order Administration)

### After each attempt, self-check against
- Did you name at least one specific constitutional provision, committee/commission, or institution rather than describing the topic generically?
- Did you connect the answer back to a Paper I theoretical concept (accountability, bureaucracy, development administration) where relevant?
- Did the conclusion offer a reasoned judgment or reform suggestion, not just a summary of facts?

## Practice Set 4: Committee/Commission and Theorist-Index Table Building (Capstone)

**Level:** Advanced | **Time:** Ongoing, one major topic per week

This is the highest-leverage practice exercise for both papers, because a single well-built index table can supply citable material for multiple questions on the same topic.

### Steps
1. Choose one major cross-cutting theme per week (accountability, civil service reform, decentralisation, financial administration, police reform — rotate to cover both papers over your prep).
2. For Paper I themes, build a one-page table: theorist/model, their one-line core claim, one piece of supporting reasoning, one standard critique.
3. For Paper II themes, build a matching table: relevant constitutional provision/amendment, relevant committee/commission and its headline recommendation, and one implementation problem or live debate.
4. Cross-reference each entry back to the specific unit and sub-topic it belongs to, so the table stays retrievable under exam conditions.
5. After building 6-8 topic tables, attempt a comparative question (e.g., "Compare the classical and human relations approaches to organisation theory," or "Compare the recommendations of successive Administrative Reforms Commissions on civil service reform") using only your own notes — no external lookups — to test how usable your notes actually are under exam conditions.

### Skill demonstrated
Synthesis of theoretical and institutional material, comparative analysis, and exam-ready recall organised by citable evidence rather than generic description.

## Practice checklist
- [ ] Completed at least 4 weeks of theorist-and-model drills across Paper I topics
- [ ] Written full timed answers for at least 10 Paper I prompts and 10 Paper II prompts
- [ ] Built at least 6 theorist-index or committee/commission-index topic tables spanning both papers
- [ ] Attempted at least one full-length, full-timing mock paper (both Paper I and Paper II)
- [ ] Self-reviewed every answer against the checklists above
