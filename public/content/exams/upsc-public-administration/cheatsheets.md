# UPSC Public Administration Optional — Quick Reference Cheatsheets

## 1. Full syllabus topic list, by paper and unit

### Paper I — Administration Theory (12 topics)
| Unit | Core topics |
|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | Meaning/scope/significance, Wilson's vision, evolution of the discipline, New Public Administration, Public Choice approach, liberalisation/privatisation/globalisation, Good Governance, New Public Management |
| 2. Administrative Thought | Scientific Management, Classical Theory, Weber's bureaucratic model and post-Weberian critique, Dynamic Administration (Follett), Human Relations School (Mayo), Functions of the Executive (Barnard), Simon's decision-making theory, Participative Management (Likert, Argyris, McGregor) |
| 3. Administrative Behaviour | Decision-making processes, communication, morale, motivation theories, leadership theories (traditional and modern) |
| 4. Organisations | Systems and contingency theories, structure and forms (Ministries, Corporations, Companies, Boards, Commissions), Headquarters-Field relationships, Regulatory Authorities, Public-Private Partnerships |

| 7. Comparative Public Administration | Historical/sociological factors, administration and politics across countries, ecology and administration, Riggsian models and critique |
| 8. Development Dynamics | Concept of development, development administration, anti-development thesis, bureaucracy and development, strong state vs. market, liberalisation's impact, women and development/self-help groups |
| 9. Personnel Administration | HR development, recruitment/training/career advancement, position classification, discipline/appraisal/promotion, pay and service conditions, grievance redressal, code of conduct, administrative ethics |
| 10. Public Policy | Policy-making models and critique, conceptualisation/planning/implementation/monitoring/evaluation, state theories |
| 11. Techniques of Administrative Improvement | Organisation and methods, work study, e-governance, management tools (network analysis, MIS, PERT, CPM) |
| 12. Financial Administration | Monetary/fiscal policies, public borrowing/debt, budget types and process, financial accountability, accounts and audit |

## 2. Key administrative thinkers and theories

| Thinker | Associated with | One-line core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Woodrow Wilson | Founding of Public Administration as a discipline (1887) | Politics-administration dichotomy — administration deserves its own systematic, non-partisan, business-like study |
| Frederick Taylor | Scientific Management | Efficiency through time-and-motion study, standardised "one best way" of performing a task |
| Henri Fayol | Classical/administrative management theory | 14 principles of management; identified core executive functions later expanded into POSDCORB |
| Max Weber | Bureaucratic model (legal-rational authority) | Hierarchy, merit-based appointment, written rules, career officials — the "ideal type" bureaucracy |
| Luther Gulick | POSDCORB | Named the seven core executive functions: Planning, Organising, Staffing, Directing, COordinating, Reporting, Budgeting |
| Mary Parker Follett | Dynamic Administration | Emphasised the "law of the situation," constructive conflict, and integrative power-sharing over pure hierarchy |
| Elton Mayo | Human Relations School (Hawthorne studies) | Worker morale, informal groups, and social factors affect productivity as much as formal structure |
| Chester I. Barnard | Functions of the Executive | Organisation as a cooperative system; authority depends on subordinates' acceptance, not just formal position |
| Herbert Simon | Decision-making theory | Critiqued classical "principles" as unproven proverbs; "bounded rationality" — administrators satisfice, not optimise |
| Douglas McGregor | Motivation/Participative Management | Theory X (workers dislike work, need control) vs. Theory Y (workers are self-motivated, seek responsibility) |
| Rensis Likert / Chris Argyris | Participative Management | Advocated participative, employee-centred management systems over rigid, authority-centred ones |
| Fred Riggs | Comparative Public Administration (ecological approach) | "Sala" model — administration in transitional societies blends traditional and modern features |
| A.V. Dicey | Administrative Law | Classical rule-of-law theorist; historically sceptical of a separate droit administratif for the state |

## 3. Important committees and commissions (Indian administrative reform)

| Committee/Commission | Focus |
|---|---|
| Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2nd ARC, constituted 2005) | Comprehensive review of public administration across multiple reports — RTI, crisis management, ethics in governance, personnel administration, local governance, and more; the single most cross-cutting reform body across Paper II |
| First Administrative Reforms Commission (1st ARC, 1966-70) | Earlier comprehensive administrative-reform review, predating the 2nd ARC by nearly four decades |
| Sarkaria Commission (1983) | Centre-State relations |
| National Police Commission (1977-81) | Police reform, policing structure, and police-public relations — directly referenced in the official Paper II syllabus (Unit 13) |
| Punchhi Commission (2007) | A later Centre-State relations review, following up on the Sarkaria Commission |

## 4. Constitutional provisions and amendments to know cold

| Provision/Amendment | Significance |
|---|---|
| Article 280 | Establishes the Finance Commission — Centre-State fiscal devolution |
| Seventh Schedule | Union, State, and Concurrent Lists dividing legislative subjects between Centre and States |
| Articles 148-151 | Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India |
| 42nd Amendment (1976) | Added Administrative Tribunals provision (Article 323-A) among other changes |
| 73rd Constitutional Amendment (1992) | Constitutionalised Panchayati Raj — rural local self-government, three-tier structure |
| 74th Constitutional Amendment (1992) | Constitutionalised urban local self-government — Municipalities, Nagar Panchayats, Municipal Corporations |
| Right to Information Act (2005) | Statutory (not constitutional) right enabling citizen access to government information — a core Unit 5 accountability mechanism |

## 5. Common terminology quick-glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| POSDCORB | Planning, Organising, Staffing, Directing, COordinating, Reporting, Budgeting — Gulick's seven core executive functions |
| Legal-rational authority | Weber's term for authority based on impersonal rules and formal procedure, as distinct from traditional or charismatic authority |
| Politics-administration dichotomy | Wilson's proposed separation of policy-deciding (political) from policy-executing (administrative) functions |
| Delegated legislation | Rule-making power delegated by the legislature to the executive/administrative bodies |
| Span of control | The number of subordinates a supervisor can effectively direct |
| Line and staff | "Line" officials hold direct command authority; "staff" officials provide advisory/expert support without command authority |
| Development administration | Administration oriented specifically toward planned socio-economic change, as distinct from routine regulatory administration |
| Civil service neutrality | The principle that civil servants serve any elected government impartially, regardless of political affiliation |
| Civil service activism | Civil servants exercising independent initiative or discretion beyond narrowly neutral rule-execution |
| Social audit | Community-level public scrutiny of government programme implementation and spending |
| New Public Management (NPM) | A reform movement applying private-sector, market-style management techniques (efficiency, competition, customer focus) to public administration |
| Good Governance | A normative framework (participation, transparency, accountability, rule of law, responsiveness) for evaluating and reforming administrative systems |

## 6. Revision strategy

**3-4 months before exam:**
- Finish first read-through of both papers; complete the theorist-index table (Paper I) and the committee/commission-and-constitutional-provision index (Paper II) for the high-yield units (Administrative Thought, District Administration, Civil Services, Administrative Reforms).

**1-2 months before:**
- Solve the last 10 years of PYQs for both papers under timed conditions.
- Redraw your theorist-index and committee-index tables from memory weekly.

**2-3 weeks before:**
- Only previous year papers and your own condensed notes — no new sources.
- Time at least one full mock paper per section, split across Paper I and Paper II.

**1 day before:**
- Light revision only: your theorist-index table, committee/commission-index table, and core terminology glossary — no new topics.
