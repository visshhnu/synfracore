# UPSC Public Administration Optional — Advanced (Paper II: Indian Administration)

Paper II is a set of 14 topics covering the actual machinery, institutions, and live debates of Indian public administration. This is the exact structure and topic list as given in UPSC's official syllabus.

## How the fourteen topics connect

Units 1-2 (Evolution of Indian Administration, Philosophical and Constitutional Framework) supply the historical and constitutional foundation — how Indian administration developed across the Mauryan, Mughal, and British periods, and the constitutional values (democracy, federalism, secularism, social justice) that the entire post-independence machinery is built to serve. Unit 3 (Public Sector Undertakings) is a distinct, self-contained block on the state's role as an economic actor. Units 4-6 (Union Government, Plans and Priorities, State Government) map the vertical structure of Indian government from the Centre down to the States — the actual institutional map (PMO, Cabinet Secretariat, Finance Commission, Governor, Chief Secretary) that most Paper II questions are set against. Unit 7 (District Administration) drops one level further, to the field-level generalist office (the Collector) that has to reconcile every layer above it with ground-level reality. Units 8-10 (Civil Services, Financial Management, Administrative Reforms) are the discipline's operational and reform machinery — who staffs the system, how money moves through it, and what has repeatedly been recommended to fix it. Units 11-12 (Rural Development, Urban Local Government) cover the constitutionally mandated third tier of decentralised government created by the 73rd and 74th Amendments. Units 13-14 (Law and Order Administration, Significant Issues) close with the state's coercive machinery and a deliberately open-ended "current issues" unit that rewards genuine, up-to-date administrative awareness over static syllabus recall.

**High-yield linkage to memorise:** Nearly every institutional unit (4, 6, 7, 8) is tested along the same three dimensions — constitutional/legal basis, actual structure and function, and reform debate or contemporary tension — so building one reusable three-column note template (basis / structure / debate) and re-applying it to each institution converts 14 topics of separate memorisation into one repeated structure applied repeatedly.

### How to actually study Unit 10 (Administrative Reforms) and Unit 14 (Significant Issues)

These two units are frequently under-revised because they feel like "extra current affairs" rather than fixed syllabus content — but they are exactly where UPSC's most analytically demanding questions concentrate, because they explicitly ask you to connect named committees/commissions to live administrative debates. The **Second Administrative Reforms Commission (2nd ARC, constituted 2005)** is the single most-cited reform body across Paper II — its reports touch district administration, civil service reform, RTI, crisis management, and ethics, so a working knowledge of which 2nd ARC report covers which theme pays off across at least five different Paper II units, not just Unit 10. Build a running "committee/commission index" (name, year, what it was asked to examine, its headline recommendation) rather than treating Unit 10 as a one-time topic.

## Cross-cutting exam skill: naming a constitutional provision, committee, or institution in every answer

Paper II's equivalent of History's "name a source" discipline is naming a specific constitutional article or amendment (73rd/74th Amendment, Article 280 for the Finance Commission), a specific committee or commission (2nd ARC, National Police Commission, Sarkaria Commission on Centre-State relations), or a specific institution (CAG, NHRC, Lokpal) rather than describing a governance concept in generic prose. This is the direct Paper II analogue of Paper I's "name a theorist" discipline (Intermediate section) — a strong Paper II answer routinely does both, borrowing a Paper I theoretical frame and grounding it in a named Indian institutional detail in the same paragraph.

## Study resources
- **M. Laxmikanth, "Public Administration"** — the standard revision-oriented text for Paper II's institutional detail (Union/State government machinery, civil services, financial management).
- **B.L. Fadia and Kuldeep Fadia, "Public Administration"** — comprehensive coverage of Indian administrative institutions and reform history.
- **2nd Administrative Reforms Commission reports (available on the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG) website)** — primary-source reading for Unit 10 and a citable authority across multiple units.
- **Previous Year Question papers (upsc.gov.in)** — the single most reliable guide to which institutions and committees are tested most often (the Collector's role, Finance Commission, 2nd ARC, and Panchayati Raj are consistently high-yield).
