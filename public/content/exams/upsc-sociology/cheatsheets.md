# UPSC Sociology Optional — Quick Reference Cheatsheets

## 1. Full syllabus topic list, by paper and unit/section

### Paper I — Fundamentals of Sociology (10 units)
| Unit | Core topics |
|---|---|
| 1. Sociology — The Discipline | Modernity and social changes in Europe, emergence of Sociology, scope vs. other social sciences, sociology and common sense |
| 2. Sociology as Science | Scientific method and critique, theoretical strands of research methodology, positivism and its critique, fact-value and objectivity, non-positivist methodologies |
| 3. Research Methods and Analysis | Qualitative/quantitative methods, techniques of data collection, variables, sampling, hypothesis, reliability, validity |
| 4. Sociological Thinkers | Marx (historical materialism, mode of production, alienation, class struggle), Durkheim (division of labour, social fact, suicide, religion), Weber (social action, ideal types, authority, bureaucracy, Protestant ethic), Parsons (social system, pattern variables), Merton (latent/manifest functions, conformity/deviance, reference groups), Mead (self and identity) |
| 5. Stratification and Mobility | Equality/inequality/hierarchy/exclusion/poverty/deprivation, functionalist/Marxist/Weberian stratification theories, class/status/gender/ethnicity/race, open/closed social mobility |

| 7. Politics and Society | Sociological theories of power, power elite, bureaucracy, pressure groups, political parties, nation/state/citizenship/democracy/civil society/ideology, protest/agitation/social movements/collective action/revolution |
| 8. Religion and Society | Sociological theories of religion, animism/monism/pluralism/sects/cults, secularization/revivalism/fundamentalism |
| 9. Systems of Kinship | Family/household/marriage, family types and forms, lineage and descent, patriarchy and sexual division of labour, contemporary trends |
| 10. Social Change in Modern Society | Theories of social change, development and dependency, agents of social change, education and science/technology's role |

## 2. Key thinkers, by name — Paper I

| Thinker | Core concepts | One-line core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Karl Marx | Historical materialism, mode of production, alienation, class struggle | Economic ownership structure determines society; workers are alienated from their own labour under capitalism |
| Emile Durkheim | Division of labour, mechanical/organic solidarity, social fact, anomie | Society coheres through interdependence; social facts constrain individual behaviour from outside |
| Max Weber | Social action, ideal types, authority (traditional/charismatic/legal-rational), Protestant ethic | Understanding society requires grasping the subjective meaning actors attach to their action |
| Talcott Parsons | Social system, pattern variables, structural functionalism | Society is a self-regulating system of interdependent parts working toward equilibrium |
| Robert K. Merton | Manifest/latent functions, conformity/deviance, reference groups | Social practices can have unintended (latent) functions distinct from their stated (manifest) purpose |
| G.H. Mead | Self, "I" and "Me", symbolic interactionism | The self develops through social interaction and internalising others' perspectives |

## 3. Key thinkers on Indian society — Paper II

| Thinker | Perspective / concept | One-line core idea |
|---|---|---|
| G.S. Ghurye | Indology | Indian society is best understood through its Sanskritic textual tradition and pan-Indian cultural unity |
| M.N. Srinivas | Structural functionalism; Sanskritisation, dominant caste, westernisation | Field-based village studies reveal locally variable status mobility, not just fixed textual hierarchy |
| A.R. Desai | Marxist sociology | Indian social structures (including caste rigidity) were shaped and hardened by colonial class exploitation |
| Louis Dumont | Homo Hierarchicus | Caste is fundamentally organised around the ideological opposition of purity and pollution |
| Andre Beteille | Caste, Class and Power | In modern India, caste, class, and power are increasingly separable rather than perfectly overlapping dimensions |
| I.P. Desai / A.M. Shah | Family studies | "Functional jointness" often persists even where households are structurally nuclear/separated |

## 4. Common terminology quick-glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sanskritisation | A lower caste's adoption of the customs/rituals of a higher caste to claim upward status (Srinivas) |
| Dominant caste | A caste that is numerically strong and economically/politically powerful in a local area, regardless of ritual rank (Srinivas) |
| Mechanical vs. organic solidarity | Durkheim's contrast between cohesion-through-sameness (traditional) and cohesion-through-interdependence (industrial) |
| Anomie | A state of normlessness where social norms lose their regulating power over individual behaviour (Durkheim) |
| Verstehen | Interpretive understanding of the subjective meaning behind social action (Weber) |
| Alienation | The worker's separation from the process, product, and fellow workers of their own labour under capitalism (Marx) |
| Latent/manifest function | Unintended/hidden vs. stated/intended purpose of a social practice (Merton) |
| Relative deprivation | A sense of disadvantage arising from comparison with a reference group, often cited in explaining social movements |
| Jajmani system | A traditional rural exchange relationship linking service-caste groups to landowning patron households |
| Sex ratio | Number of females per 1,000 males in a population — a key demographic and gender-equity indicator |
| Secularization | The declining social significance of religion in public institutional life, as distinct from personal religiosity |

## 5. Revision strategy

**2-3 months before exam:**
- Finish first read-through of both papers; complete the six-thinker one-pagers (Paper I) and named-perspective tables for caste/family/class (Paper II).

**1 month before:**
- Solve the last 10 years of PYQs for both papers under timed conditions.
- Redraw the thinker-to-topic mapping from memory weekly.

**1 week before:**
- Only previous year papers and your own condensed notes — no new sources.
- Time at least one full mock paper per section.

**1 day before:**
- Light revision only: your six-thinker one-pagers, named-perspective tables, and core terminology glossary — no new topics.
