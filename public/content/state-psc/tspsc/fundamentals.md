# TSPSC Prep — Fundamentals

## Group I-IV — structural differences

Group I follows the full three-stage state-PSC pattern (Preliminary, Mains, Interview), while Groups II-IV use progressively simpler processes without a comparably deep Mains/Interview stage — the same structural pattern established across this technology cluster's TNPSC, KPSC, MPSC, and APPSC material, applied here to Telangana's Group classifications.

## Telugu language paper

Most TSPSC exams test **Telugu language** proficiency — comprehension, grammar, and composition — reflecting Telugu's role as Telangana's administrative and educational medium, shared with Andhra Pradesh given the common language and much shared pre-2014 literary heritage (this academy's APPSC technology covers the same underlying language paper structure from the Andhra Pradesh side). Candidates whose education wasn't primarily Telugu-medium should treat this as a dedicated preparation area, and should note that TSPSC sets its own specific passages and question patterns distinct from APPSC's.

## General Studies foundation — Telangana state board curriculum

TSPSC's General Studies content draws significantly from Telangana's own state board curriculum — established as a distinct curricular body following the state's 2014 formation, rather than continuing to rely on undivided Andhra Pradesh's pre-2014 curriculum. Candidates should specifically source current Telangana state board textbooks (not older, pre-bifurcation Andhra Pradesh board material), since TSPSC's content emphasis reflects this genuinely distinct, post-2014 curricular framework. `(needs verification — recheck against current source: current TSPSC syllabus specifics and curricular sources are periodically clarified/revised by the commission.)`

## Why the Telangana movement requires dedicated preparation attention

Fundamentals' most important TSPSC-specific point: the Telangana movement (Overview) isn't simply one historical topic among many — it's treated as foundational context for understanding Telangana's entire post-2014 administrative and political character, meaning candidates should approach it with the same seriousness as core General Studies content, not as a peripheral current-affairs item. Candidates preparing primarily from generic South Indian history material (or material originally written for undivided Andhra Pradesh) commonly under-prepare this specific, high-weight topic.

## Getting started

1. Identify whether you're targeting Group I (full three-stage process) or Groups II-IV (simpler processes) before structuring preparation depth.
2. Source current Telangana state board textbooks specifically, distinct from older undivided-Andhra-Pradesh curricular material.
3. Treat the Telangana movement as core, high-weight preparation content requiring dedicated study — not a minor current-affairs topic to review briefly alongside other material.
