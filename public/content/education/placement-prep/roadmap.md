# Placement Preparation — Learning Roadmap

## Estimated Time to Placement-Ready
**8-12 weeks**, depending heavily on target company tier — service companies (TCS, Infosys) realistically need 2-4 weeks of focused aptitude/basic-coding prep, while FAANG/top product companies need 3-6 months of DSA-heavy preparation. This roadmap assumes a general placement-season timeline; adjust the phase lengths based on your actual target companies (see this course's Overview for company-wise preparation focus).

## Phase 1: Foundation and Resume (Week 1-2)

- Understand the full hiring funnel: resume screening → online assessment → technical rounds → HR round → offer
- Build and audit your resume — one page, ATS-friendly, STAR-format project bullets with measurable results
- Complete Project 1 (resume build-and-audit) from this course's Projects section

**Checkpoint:** does every project bullet on your resume state a measurable result, not just a description of what was built?

## Phase 2: Aptitude and Reasoning (Week 2-5)

- Quantitative aptitude: time & work, time-speed-distance, percentages, probability, permutations & combinations — and speed (70-90 seconds per question is the real target)
- Logical reasoning: syllogisms, blood relations, directions, puzzles
- Verbal ability: reading comprehension, error identification
- Complete Project 2 (timed aptitude practice-test cycle) from this course's Projects section — run at least 3 full cycles, not one test

**Checkpoint:** across your practice-test cycles, is your score trending upward, and can you name your specific weakest topic (not just "quant" generally)?

## Phase 3: Coding Round and Technical Interview Prep (Week 5-9)

- Coding round patterns matched to target company difficulty (service companies: easy loops/arrays/strings; product companies: medium-to-hard DSA)
- Constraint-to-complexity mapping (n ≤ 10⁵ → O(n log n), etc.) — a genuinely high-value skill for solving unfamiliar problems under time pressure
- CS fundamentals commonly tested: OOP concepts, OS (process vs. thread, deadlock), DBMS (normalization, indexing), networks (TCP vs. UDP, REST)
- Run at least one full mock technical interview from this course's Projects section (Project 3)

**Checkpoint:** given a problem's constraints, can you correctly predict the expected time complexity before starting to solve it?

## Phase 4: HR Round and Company-Specific Prep (Week 9-12)

- HR round preparation: "tell me about yourself" as a 2-minute pitch (not a life story), researched "why this company" answers, and a genuine self-aware weakness answer
- Salary negotiation basics — researching market rate, never naming a number first
- Complete Project 3's HR-round mock interviews and review this course's Interview Q&A section
- Tailor final prep to your specific target companies' known focus areas (see this course's Overview for the company-tier breakdown)

## Skills You'll Build

| Skill Area | What You'll Learn |
|---|---|
| Resume Craft | ATS optimization and measurable, STAR-format achievement writing |
| Aptitude & Reasoning | Speed and accuracy across quant, logical, and verbal sections |
| Technical Interviewing | DSA problem-solving under time pressure, with clear communication |
| CS Fundamentals | The recurring OOP/OS/DBMS/Networks questions across technical rounds |
| HR & Behavioral | Structured, specific answers instead of generic or cliché ones |

## Weekly Study Plan

```
Monday:    Aptitude/reasoning practice (timed)
Tuesday:   DSA practice problems matched to your target company tier
Wednesday: CS fundamentals review (OOP/OS/DBMS/Networks, rotating topics)
Thursday:  Mock interview practice (technical one week, HR the next)
Friday:    Resume/profile review and company-specific research
Weekend:   A full timed mock aptitude test, or a longer DSA practice session
```

## Red Flags to Avoid

- ❌ Applying to companies without researching their actual interview focus — service companies and FAANG test very different things, and prep should reflect that
- ❌ Treating the HR round as a formality — it can and does eliminate candidates, especially on communication and genuine self-awareness
- ❌ Practicing aptitude questions untimed — the real test has strict time pressure, and untimed practice doesn't build that specific skill
- ❌ Generic resume bullets ("worked on a project") instead of specific, measurable ones
- ❌ Only preparing DSA and neglecting CS fundamentals — OOP/OS/DBMS/Networks questions show up constantly in technical rounds, especially round 2 onward

## Getting Placement-Ready

1. **Documented practice artifacts**: the 3 projects in this course's Projects section — a resume audit trail, an aptitude score-improvement log, and a mock-interview log with tracked improvement
2. **Target-company-specific prep**: match your DSA difficulty, CS-fundamentals depth, and timeline to your actual target companies, not a generic "prep for everything equally" approach
3. **Timed practice throughout**: aptitude tests, coding rounds, and even mock interviews should be run under real time constraints, not casually
4. **Know your own resume and projects cold**: "explain this project," "how would you scale this," and "why this technology" are near-universal follow-up questions — be ready with real, specific answers
5. **Treat the HR round with the same seriousness as technical rounds**: specific, researched, self-aware answers consistently outperform generic ones
