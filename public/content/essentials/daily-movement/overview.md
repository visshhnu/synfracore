# Daily Movement and Exercise Overview

**Before you start:** no prior fitness background is needed — this page is aimed at non-athletes building a sustainable movement habit. *(Note: specific figures below — percentages, minutes, thresholds — reflect commonly cited exercise-science reference points; treat them as general guidance, not personalized medical advice.)*

## Why This Exists (The Hook)

"Sitting is the new smoking" sounds like an exaggeration until you learn the specific finding behind it: sitting 8+ hours daily is an independent risk factor for early death — independent meaning even people who exercise regularly still carry extra risk from the sitting itself. That's counterintuitive: a 30-minute workout doesn't cancel out 8 sedentary hours the way most people assume. This page exists to explain why movement throughout the day matters separately from a dedicated workout, and what the minimum effective dose actually looks like.

**Analogy** — Think of daily movement like watering a plant a little every day, not flooding it once a week. A plant watered once a week with a huge amount doesn't thrive the way one watered a little each day does — the soil can only absorb so much at once, and the plant needs consistent access, not a periodic flood. Your body's circulation, insulin sensitivity, and joint health respond the same way — frequent small movement (standing every 2 hours, a short walk) delivers benefits a single intense weekly workout, however good, structurally can't replace on its own.

**Try it (2 minutes)** — Reason through why the 20-20-20-2 rule specifies BOTH a 20-second eye break every 20 minutes AND a 2-minute movement break every 2 hours, rather than just one rule, without looking anything up: eye strain from staring at a close screen and circulation problems from staying seated are two genuinely different physiological issues, on two different timescales — eye muscles fatigue in minutes, while blood pooling in the legs from prolonged sitting builds up over hours. Why would a single rule addressing only one of these problems (say, only eye breaks) leave the other risk (circulation) completely unaddressed?

## Why Movement is Medicine

Sitting 8+ hours daily is an independent risk factor for early death even with separate exercise. The body evolved for constant low-level movement.

**Key benefits:**
- Reduces all-cause mortality by 30-35%
- Improves insulin sensitivity and reduces type 2 diabetes risk
- Increases BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) for better memory and focus
- Reduces mild-to-moderate depression symptoms as effectively as antidepressants
- Improves sleep quality and deepens slow-wave sleep

## WHO Minimum Guidelines

| Type | Minimum per Week |
|------|-----------------|
| Moderate aerobic (brisk walk, cycling) | 150 minutes |
| Vigorous aerobic (running, HIIT) | 75 minutes |
| Strength training | 2 sessions (all major muscle groups) |

**20-20-20-2 Rule:**
- Every 20 minutes: look 20 feet away for 20 seconds (eye strain)
- Every 2 hours: stand and move for 2 minutes (circulation)

**Daily movement habits:**
- Morning: 5-minute stretch (hip flexors, spinal mobility)
- Lunch: 15-minute walk outside (sunlight + circulation)
- Evening: 30 minutes of any exercise you enjoy

## 20-Minute Home Workout (No Equipment)

```
Warm-up (3 min): Jumping jacks, arm circles
Circuit (14 min, 2 rounds):
  Push-ups: 15 reps
  Bodyweight squats: 20 reps
  Plank: 30 seconds
  Glute bridges: 15 reps
Cooldown (3 min): Static stretches, 30 sec each
```

## Signs You Need to Move More

- Afternoon energy crashes (2-4pm)
- Back or hip pain after sitting
- Difficulty concentrating after 1 hour desk work
- Resting heart rate above 75 bpm
- Waking tired despite 7-8 hours sleep
