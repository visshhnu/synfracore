# Mental Wellness — Overview

**Before you start:** no prior background is needed. If you or someone you know is in immediate crisis or having thoughts of self-harm, contact a local emergency service or a crisis helpline right away — this page is educational, not a substitute for professional care.

Mental health is your emotional, psychological, and social well-being — it shapes how you think, feel, act, handle stress, and relate to other people. It is not a fixed state you either "have" or "don't have"; like physical health, everyone has mental health, and it moves along a spectrum over time rather than sitting in one of two boxes.

## Why This Exists (The Hook)

Physical health has an obvious feedback loop — a cut bleeds, a fever spikes, pain tells you something is wrong right now. Mental health rarely announces itself that clearly. Chronic stress, poor sleep, and social isolation build up gradually, often reframed as "just being busy" or "just how I am," until the accumulated effect shows up as burnout, anxiety, or depression — at which point it feels sudden, even though it wasn't. Understanding the basics here is what lets you notice the gradual signal instead of only the eventual crisis.

**Analogy** — Think of mental health like a bank account with both deposits and withdrawals, not a fixed balance you're born with. Sleep, exercise, social connection, and a sense of purpose are deposits; chronic stress, isolation, and unprocessed emotional strain are withdrawals. Nobody goes bankrupt from one single bad day — it's a sustained pattern of more withdrawals than deposits that eventually overdraws the account. The self-care basics below aren't indulgences; they're the deposits that keep the account solvent.

None of these are character flaws — they arise from a genuine mix of biological factors (genetics, brain chemistry) and environmental ones (stress, relationships, life circumstances). Roughly 1 in 4 people experience a mental health condition at some point in their life.

## The Bio-Psycho-Social Model

Mental health isn't caused by any single factor — it's shaped by three interacting categories at once:

**Protective factors** (things that help): strong social connections, a sense of purpose, physical health (sleep, exercise, nutrition), coping skills, and access to professional support when needed.

**Risk factors** (things that strain): social isolation, financial stress, trauma history, substance misuse, chronic physical illness, and family history of mental illness.

## Evidence-Based Self-Care

- **Sleep** — 7-9 hours for adults, consistent sleep/wake times (even on weekends), no screens 30-60 minutes before bed. See [Sleep & Recovery](/academies/essentials/sleep-health/overview) for the full picture.
- **Physical activity** — 150 minutes/week of moderate intensity exercise; even a 10-minute walk measurably improves mood.
- **Social connection** — strong relationships are the single strongest predictor of long-term happiness; quality matters more than quantity.
- **Stress management** — deep breathing (4-7-8: in for 4, hold for 7, out for 8) activates the parasympathetic nervous system; progressive muscle relaxation, journaling, and mindfulness all have real evidence behind them.

## When to Seek Professional Help

Professional help is warranted when symptoms persist more than 2 weeks, start affecting daily functioning (work, relationships), involve thoughts of self-harm, or when substances are being used to cope.

| Who | What they do |
|---|---|
| Therapist / Psychologist | Talk therapy — CBT, DBT, and related approaches |
| Psychiatrist | Medication management (a doctor specializing in mental health) |
| GP / Family doctor | First point of contact, can refer you further |
