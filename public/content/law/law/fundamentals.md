# Law & Compliance — Fundamentals

## Why legal literacy matters even if you're not a lawyer

Every time you sign an employment contract, rent an apartment, buy something online, or use the internet, the law is already determining your rights and obligations — whether or not you're aware of them. Most people discover they had a specific legal right only after they needed it and didn't know to use it. This section is deliberately about awareness, not legal advice: for any specific matter, an actual qualified advocate is the right resource — the goal here is knowing enough to recognize when that's needed and to not sign away rights unknowingly in the meantime.

## Criminal law basics — IPC/BNS, and what changed in 2024

India replaced its colonial-era criminal codes with three new laws in 2024:

| Old law (replaced) | New law | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Indian Penal Code (IPC) 1860 | Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) 2023 | Criminal offences and punishments |
| Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) 1973 | Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) 2023 | Arrest, trial, bail procedure |
| Indian Evidence Act 1872 | Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023 | Rules of evidence in court |

The specific rights worth knowing before you ever need them, not after:
- **You have the right to file an FIR** for a cognisable offence — police cannot lawfully refuse to register one; if they do, you can escalate to the Superintendent of Police or file directly in court
- **Bail is a right, not a favor, for bailable offences** — for non-bailable offences it's at the court's discretion
- **Article 22 guarantees the right to legal counsel** for every arrested person, with state-provided legal aid if you can't afford one
- **Article 20 protects you from self-incrimination** — you cannot be compelled to be a witness against yourself, and you have the right to remain silent

## Civil law: contracts and property, the fundamentals

A valid contract under the Indian Contract Act 1872 requires six elements together — **offer + acceptance + consideration + free consent + capacity + lawful object**. Missing any one makes the contract void or voidable; consent obtained through coercion, fraud, or misrepresentation specifically makes a contract voidable at the affected party's choice, not automatically void.

For property specifically, the distinction that causes the most real disputes: a **Sale Agreement is only a promise to sell**; a **registered Sale Deed is the actual legal transfer**. Paying the full amount against a sale agreement alone, before a registered deed exists, is a common and costly mistake — always confirm registration at the Sub-Registrar's office, and pull an encumbrance certificate to confirm the property carries no existing loans or disputes before paying anything substantial.

## Employment law: the rights that exist by default

Four Labour Codes (2019-2020) are meant to eventually consolidate 29 existing labour laws, but implementation has been gradual — in the meantime, the older individual laws remain the operative ones:

- **Gratuity** (Payment of Gratuity Act 1972) — a legal entitlement, not a discretionary bonus, after 5 continuous years with one employer: `(last basic salary × 15 × years) ÷ 26`, payable within 30 days of resignation
- **EPF** (EPF Act 1952) — 12% of basic salary from you, 12% from your employer, into your PF account; check contributions against your UAN on the EPFO portal, since non-deposit by an employer is a criminal offence, not just a civil dispute
- **POSH Act 2013** — every organization with 10+ employees is legally required to maintain an Internal Complaints Committee; complaints must be filed within 3 months of the incident
- **Maternity Benefit Act 1961** — 26 weeks of paid leave at organizations with 10+ employees, with protection from termination during that leave

Before signing an offer letter, the clauses worth actually reading rather than skimming: **notice period** (what you owe, and what the employer owes you if they terminate without cause), **non-compete** (widely written into Indian contracts but largely unenforceable against employees in court — NDAs covering genuine confidential information are a separate matter and remain enforceable), and **moonlighting/IP assignment** language, which varies far more between employers than most candidates expect.

## Intellectual property, in brief

| IP type | Protects | Duration | Registration needed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copyright | Creative works — books, code, music, film | Life of author + 60 years | Automatic on creation |
| Patent | New, non-obvious inventions | 20 years from filing | Yes, at the Patent Office |
| Trademark | Brand name, logo, slogan | 10 years, renewable | Yes, at the Trademark Registry |
| Trade secret | Confidential business information | As long as kept secret | No — protected via NDAs/access control |

For anyone writing software specifically: your code is automatically copyrighted the moment you write it, with no registration required for that baseline protection. What you write as an employee, though, typically belongs to the employer by default — the actual scope of that assignment lives in your employment contract's IP clause, which is exactly why that clause is worth reading closely rather than assuming a standard boilerplate meaning.

## Cyber/IT law — the baseline every internet user is already subject to

The **IT Act 2000** (amended 2008) is India's primary cyber law, covering electronic contracts, digital signatures, and a range of cybercrime offences — identity theft (Section 66C), cheating by impersonation online (Section 66D), and privacy violations like non-consensual image sharing (Section 66E) among them. The **DPDP Act 2023** is India's comprehensive data privacy law layered on top of this: it establishes a data principal's rights to know what personal data is held, correct it, request its erasure, and seek grievance redress, with companies required to obtain explicit, specific consent before processing personal data — not a buried, pre-checked box in a terms-of-service page.
