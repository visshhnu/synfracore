# Legal Career Growth — Junior Practice to Independent Practice

## The Arc of a Litigation Career

Enrollment (AIBE) gets you the right to practice. What actually builds a career happens in the years after — and it follows a fairly predictable arc: junior practice under a senior → building a specialisation → running an independent practice. This page covers all three stages together because they are sequential steps of one continuous progression, not separate subjects.

## Stage 1 — Junior Advocate Practice (Years 1–4)

- **Chamber practice**: most new advocates join a senior's chambers — you draft, research, and attend court as a junior.
- **Court procedure fluency**: filing, cause lists, adjournments, court etiquette — none of this is taught in law school, it's learned by doing.
- **Drafting**: plaints, written statements, applications, bail petitions — volume and repetition build competence faster than theory.
- **Reality check**: the first 2–3 years are usually the lowest-earning. Most advocates supplement chamber work with part-time drafting or legal process outsourcing (LPO) work.

## Stage 2 — Building a Specialisation (Years 3–8)

Generalists plateau early; specialists compound. Common specialisation paths in India:

| Specialisation | What it involves | Typical entry point |
|---|---|---|
| Criminal law | Bail, trial, appeals | District court litigation |
| Corporate/commercial | Contracts, M&A support, compliance | In-house or law firm associate |
| Consumer protection | Consumer Protection Act 2019, NCDRC/State/District Commissions | Growing area, lower entry barrier |
| Family law | Divorce, custody, maintenance | District court practice |
| Constitutional/writ practice | High Court and Supreme Court writs | Usually after several years of grounding |

Pick a specialisation based on: which court you have access to, what work is actually available locally, and — honestly — what kind of client interactions you can sustain for a decade.

## Stage 3 — Independent Practice (Year 5+)

- **Building a client base**: referrals from your senior, from other advocates, and from your specialisation's professional network are the primary channel — not advertising (which is restricted under Bar Council rules).
- **Setting up**: your own chamber or a shared office, a clerk/junior of your own, a fee structure (per-appearance, retainer, or case-based).
- **Financial reality**: independent practice income is irregular for the first 1–2 years of transition. Most advocates keep some referral work from their former senior during this period.
- **Bar association involvement**: joining local bar association activities builds the professional network that sustains a referral-based practice long-term.

:::info Why these three stages share one page
Junior practice, specialisation, and independent practice are not three unrelated topics — they are one continuous career progression where each stage sets up the next. Splitting them into three thin pages would fragment a narrative that's genuinely sequential.
:::
