# Legal Career Growth — Independent Practice (Year 5+)

## Making the Transition

Moving from a senior's chambers to an independent practice is usually gradual, not a single cutover date. Most advocates keep taking some referral work from their former senior while slowly building their own client base.

- **Building a client base**: referrals from your senior, from other advocates, and from your specialisation's professional network are the primary channel — not advertising, which is restricted under Bar Council of India rules.
- **Setting up**: your own chamber or a shared office, a clerk/junior of your own, and a fee structure (per-appearance, retainer, or case-based).
- **Financial reality**: independent practice income is irregular for the first 1–2 years of the transition — budget for this rather than being surprised by it.
- **Bar association involvement**: joining local bar association activities and committees builds the professional network that sustains a referral-based practice long-term.

## Fee Structures in Independent Practice

| Structure | How it works | Common for |
|---|---|---|
| Per-appearance | Fixed fee per court date | Routine matters, mentions, adjournments |
| Case-based / lump sum | One fee for the entire matter through to disposal | Straightforward civil/criminal matters |
| Retainer | Monthly fee for ongoing availability | Corporate clients, recurring business matters |
| Contingency-adjacent arrangements | Fee tied loosely to outcome (not pure contingency, which has ethical limits in India) | Rare, mostly in commercial disputes |

## Growing Beyond Solo Practice

- Taking on your own juniors once volume justifies it — this is the point where you start training others the way your senior trained you.
- Building a small chamber (2–4 advocates) to share overheads and cover each other's court dates.
- Diversifying into arbitration, mediation panels, or tribunal practice as alternate/parallel revenue streams once litigation practice is established.
- Long-term reputational compounding: judges, opposing counsel, and clients all remember advocates who are reliably prepared — this is what eventually converts into a steady, less referral-dependent practice.
