# Civil Law — CPC, Contract Act & Transfer of Property

## Why This Page Exists Separately

The Judiciary Exams page covers the **exam process** (stages, recruitment tiers, state-wise patterns). This page covers the **substantive law** those exams actually test — the Code of Civil Procedure, the Indian Contract Act, and the Transfer of Property Act. They're deliberately kept separate: one is "how the exam works," the other is "what you need to know."

## Code of Civil Procedure (CPC), 1908

- **Structure**: Sections 1–158 (the body) + Orders I–LI (the procedural detail) in the First Schedule.
- **Key concepts civil-judge aspirants must know cold**:
  - Jurisdiction (Sections 9, 15–20) — which court can hear which suit.
  - Res judicata (Section 11) — a matter already decided can't be re-litigated.
  - Framing of issues (Order XIV) — the backbone of how a civil trial proceeds.
  - Interim orders — temporary injunctions (Order XXXIX), attachment before judgment (Order XXXVIII).
  - Execution of decrees (Order XXI) — how a winning party actually gets relief enforced.

## Indian Contract Act, 1872

- **Formation**: offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity to contract (Sections 10–30).
- **Void and voidable contracts**: coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, mistake.
- **Performance and breach**: Sections 37–67 — discharge of contract, damages, quantum meruit.
- **Special contracts**: indemnity, guarantee, bailment, pledge, agency (Sections 124–238) — frequently tested in judiciary prelims.

## Transfer of Property Act (TP Act), 1882

- **Core transfers**: sale, mortgage, lease, exchange, gift — definitions and essential conditions for each.
- **Doctrine of lis pendens** (Section 52) — property under litigation can't be transferred to defeat the suit's outcome.
- **Doctrine of part performance** (Section 53A) — protects a transferee who has taken possession under an unregistered contract.

## How to Study This for Judiciary Exams

1. Build a **section-number memory map** first — judiciary MCQs often quote a section number directly.
2. Practice **problem-based questions** (fact pattern → which section applies) rather than pure definition recall — that's the actual exam format.
3. Cross-reference with the Judiciary Exams page for exam-specific weightage and previous state-wise question patterns.

:::tip
Civil Judge mains papers almost always include a judgment-writing question built on a CPC or Contract Act fact pattern — mastering the substantive law here directly improves your Mains score, not just Prelims.
:::
