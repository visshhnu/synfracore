# Judiciary Exams FAQ

**Q: Do I need any experience to apply for Civil Judge?**
A: No — most states do not require advocacy experience for Civil Judge (Jr Division). An LLB degree plus age eligibility is sufficient. Only District Judge (Direct) recruitment requires 7 years' advocacy experience.

**Q: What is the age limit?**
A: Typically 21–35 years for Civil Judge (Jr Division), and up to 45 years for District Judge (Direct). Age relaxation applies for SC/ST (5 years), OBC (3 years), PwD (10 years), and in-service candidates (varies by state). Always check the state-specific notification.

**Q: Can someone who studied in Hindi medium succeed in judiciary exams?**
A: Absolutely. Most state judiciary exams offer optional papers in Hindi and regional languages. Mains can usually be answered in Hindi or English, and the judgment-writing exercise also has a Hindi option in most states. Language should not be a barrier.

**Q: Is coaching necessary for judiciary exams?**
A: Not mandatory, but helpful for structured preparation, test series, and mock interviews. Many successful candidates clear through self-study using standard textbooks. Online test series (₹3,000–₹10,000/year) provide structured practice without expensive classroom coaching.

**Q: How many times can I attempt judiciary exams?**
A: Unlimited attempts within the age limit. Most states do not restrict the number of attempts — only age is the bar.

**Q: What happens after selection?**
A: Selected candidates undergo training at the State Judicial Academy for 1–2 years, then are posted as Civil Judge / Judicial Magistrate. Incremental salary increases and promotions are based on performance plus seniority. Elevation to the High Court bench is possible for outstanding judges, via the collegium system.

**Q: Is UP PCS J the toughest judiciary exam?**
A: UP PCS J is considered among the most competitive, due to the sheer number of applicants (2–4 lakh applications for roughly 300–500 posts). Delhi and Maharashtra exams are also highly competitive. Syllabus and difficulty are broadly similar across states — competition level is what actually varies.

**Q: What is the salary after joining?**
A: Civil Judge (Jr Division): approximately ₹77,840 basic + DA/HRA, totaling roughly ₹1.1–1.5 lakh/month all-in, plus official residence, vehicle allowance, and security. Pension on retirement, annual increments, and promotion to Additional District Judge after DPC (Departmental Promotion Committee review).

**Q: Can judiciary officers be transferred to other states?**
A: No — state judicial service officers serve within their state's cadre. An All India Judicial Service (AIJS) has been proposed but not implemented. High Court and Supreme Court judges can be transferred between courts by collegium recommendation, which is a separate process.

**Q: What is the difference between a Judicial Magistrate and an Executive Magistrate?**
A: A Judicial Magistrate is part of the judiciary, appointed by/under the High Court, and handles criminal cases (trials, bail, remand). An Executive Magistrate is part of the executive, appointed under the state government (e.g. SDM, Tehsildar), and handles administrative orders (Section 144 CrPC, maintenance orders). Both are called "Magistrates" but serve very different functions.
