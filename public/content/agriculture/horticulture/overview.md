# Horticulture and Crop Production — Overview

## What horticulture covers

Horticulture is the branch of agriculture dealing with intensively cultivated plants grown for food, ornament, and landscaping — fruit crops, vegetable crops, and floriculture (flower/ornamental cultivation) — plus the post-harvest handling that determines how much of what's grown actually reaches a usable, sellable state. It's distinct from field-crop agronomy (cereals, pulses, oilseeds grown at scale) mainly in intensity: horticultural crops generally need more per-plant management (pruning, training, pest monitoring, careful harvest timing) and deliver higher value per unit area in return.

## Why it matters economically

India is among the world's largest producers of fruits and vegetables by volume, and horticulture as a sector now contributes a larger share of agricultural GDP than food grains in many states, despite occupying less cultivated area — a direct consequence of horticultural crops' higher value per hectare. This economic weight is exactly why horticulture is a distinct, heavily-tested subject in agricultural competitive exams rather than a minor subtopic under general agronomy.

## What this technology covers

- **Fruit crops** — classification (tropical, subtropical, temperate), propagation, and orchard management.
- **Vegetable crops** — classification by plant part used, nursery raising, and cultivation practices.
- **Floriculture** — ornamental and cut-flower cultivation, an increasingly commercial segment.
- **Post-harvest management** — the handling, storage, and processing stage between harvest and final sale, covered in depth in Advanced, since post-harvest losses are one of the sector's biggest, most exam-relevant problems.

## Why post-harvest management gets special emphasis

A meaningful share of India's horticultural produce is lost between harvest and consumption — not from poor cultivation, but from inadequate cold chain, storage, and handling infrastructure. This makes post-harvest technology a genuinely high-stakes, frequently tested topic distinct from production itself: a technically excellent harvest can still result in significant economic loss if post-harvest handling is inadequate, which is precisely why this technology treats post-harvest as a core pillar rather than an afterthought tacked onto production topics.

## Exam and career relevance

Horticulture is core syllabus for ICAR entrance/JRF, IBPS AFO, NABARD Grade A, state horticulture department recruitment, and Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) scientist positions — commonly tested alongside soil science and agri-business, since real horticultural decision-making draws on all three.

## How to use this technology's sections

Fundamentals covers classification and propagation — the vocabulary and basic techniques everything else builds on. Intermediate applies these to specific worked techniques (grafting, nursery raising, protected cultivation). Advanced covers post-harvest technology, high-density planting, and export-oriented horticulture. Interview and Cheatsheets provide exam-format practice and quick reference.
