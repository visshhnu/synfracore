# Food Processing & Agri-Business — Fundamentals

Overview introduced the different scales of food processing business and flagged a real naming trap in scheme acronyms. This page covers the actual licensing steps and how to approach financing without hardcoding numbers that regulators can and do revise.

## Step 1 — FSSAI licensing is the real gating step, not an afterthought

Every food business in India needs an FSSAI (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India) registration or license — this is not optional and not skippable regardless of scale. The tier depends on annual turnover:

```
FSSAI license tiers (verified current as of this page's last review --
these thresholds were themselves revised effective 1 April 2026, which
is exactly the kind of regulatory change this academy tracks rather
than assumes stays fixed):

  Basic Registration -- turnover up to Rs. 1.5 crore
  State License       -- turnover Rs. 1.5 crore to Rs. 50 crore
  Central License      -- turnover above Rs. 50 crore

Note: certain categories of food business must obtain a State or
Central license regardless of turnover, irrespective of how small the
business actually is -- check the FoSCoS (Food Safety Compliance
System) eligibility matrix for your specific product category before
assuming Basic Registration applies just because your turnover is low.
```

**(needs verification — recheck against current source)**: FSSAI thresholds and validity rules are genuinely revised periodically (this page's April 2026 threshold revision is itself an example) — confirm current thresholds directly at fssai.gov.in or the FoSCoS portal before applying, not from this page alone.

## Step 2 — Other required registrations (applies broadly, not just to food)

- **Udyam (MSME) registration** — free, online, and required for most food-processing-specific financing schemes covered in Step 3.
- **GST registration** — mandatory above the relevant turnover threshold, and needed in practice for most B2B supply relationships regardless.
- **State Pollution Control Board consent/NOC** — required for processing units above a certain scale or with specific effluent/waste profiles (dal mills and rice mills, for instance, generate husk and byproduct that need a disposal or use plan) — the exact requirement is unit-type and state-specific, not universal.

## Step 3 — Financing: three genuinely different schemes, correctly named

Overview flagged a real naming trap. Naming each precisely here, rather than by ambiguous acronym alone:

| Scheme | What it actually is | Scale it targets |
|---|---|---|
| **PMFME** (PM Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises) | Credit-linked subsidy, capacity building, and marketing/branding support | Micro-scale units specifically |
| **PM Kisan SAMPADA Yojana** | Comprehensive infrastructure scheme — food parks, cold chain, agro-processing clusters | Larger-scale, infrastructure-heavy projects |
| **Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana** (also abbreviated PMKSY) | Irrigation and water-security scheme | Not a food-processing scheme at all — do not apply here expecting processing-business support |
| **NABARD Food Processing Fund** | Direct term loans (up to 75% of project cost) specifically for units in Designated Food Parks | Mid-to-large scale, DFP-located units |

**Do not rely on this table's current-status/amount details for an actual application** — check the **[Scheme Navigator](/schemes)**, filtered to "Food Processing," for each scheme's individually-verified current status, amounts, and a direct link to the official source before applying.

## Try It (2 minutes)

A prospective food processing unit expects Rs. 3 crore in annual turnover. Using the verified thresholds above, which FSSAI license tier applies? You should land on: State License (turnover between Rs. 1.5 crore and Rs. 50 crore) — assuming the specific product category doesn't fall under one of the categories requiring a higher tier regardless of turnover, which the FoSCoS matrix would need to confirm before finalizing.
