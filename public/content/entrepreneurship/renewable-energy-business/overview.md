# Renewable Energy Business — Overview

**Before you start:** this is a business-starting guide, not a solar/electrical engineering course — it covers what it takes to start and run a renewable-energy installation or servicing business, not how photovoltaic cells or inverters work.

## What this technology covers

"Renewable energy business" in the Indian context most commonly means one of these:

Each of these is a real, distinct business model with different customer relationships, sales cycles, and capital needs — this technology treats them separately rather than as one undifferentiated "solar business."

## Why this sector, specifically

Solar and renewable-energy installation is physical, site-specific, and requires hands-on electrical work and ongoing maintenance relationships — none of which a purely digital business needs to plan for. It's also a sector with substantial, genuinely active government financing and demand-support infrastructure (PM-KUSUM, IREDA, dedicated SIDBI financing), specifically because renewable energy adoption is a stated national priority — which again is exactly why this technology points to a live-tracked navigator for scheme specifics rather than treating any one scheme's terms as permanent.

**Analogy** — Think of the four renewable-energy business models like four different roles in a single construction project: the EPC contractor designs and builds the whole system (highest capital and complexity, but captures the most value per project), the PM-KUSUM empanelled vendor works within a government-run tender process (lower sales-cycle cost since the government does the demand generation, but tighter margins and state-specific compliance), the rooftop installer sells directly to individual residential/commercial clients (a genuine sales business, not just a technical one), and the O&M provider is the ongoing facilities manager (lowest capital, but success depends entirely on relationships built during the initial installations, whether your own or someone else's).

## A real, current example of why timing matters in this sector

PM-KUSUM's current implementation phase has an extended deadline of 30 September 2026 — essentially now, by the time this is read. A follow-on phase ("PM-KUSUM 2.0," reportedly adding a dedicated agrivoltaics component) has been publicly described by the responsible minister as imminent, but had **not been formally launched** as of the most recent verification. A business built around PM-KUSUM empanelment needs to track this transition directly — via the [Scheme Navigator](/schemes) — rather than assume the current phase's terms simply continue unchanged into whatever comes next.

## Real-world relevance

India's renewable energy sector, particularly agricultural solar (PM-KUSUM) and rooftop solar, has substantial government demand-support behind it, which creates a genuine opportunity for installation and servicing businesses — but that demand-support is scheme-driven and, as the example above shows, subject to real phase transitions and revisions that a founder needs to track actively, not assume are settled.
